# Sprint 24 review — 27 August 2026

**Scope.** The benchmark instrument. Both findings correct, and the first is the
most embarrassing defect found in this project: the check-in was scoring, as the
correct answer, the exact reasoning I removed from a mission in sprint 18.

---

## Finding 1 — the instrument could not support its own framing

### The plaque came back, as a scored item

Sprint 18 removed this from Two Answers, One Truth:

> *"The plaque, because it was put there by the people who built it."*

A plaque establishes neither. They get installed years later, by people who were
not present, saying whatever narrow thing somebody chose to engrave — and 1908
and 1961 turned out to be compatible answers about different events.

**Pre-6 was scoring the identical rationale as correct**, in the same shape:
*"The stone, because the people who built it put it there."* Same ambiguity too
— the library opening and this building going up may be different events.

That is worse than the mission version was. A mission teaches something and can
be corrected; an item marks a child down for **not** holding the belief, and
then reports the result as evidence about the class.

Pre-6 is replaced. Same reasoning as post-6 — which source is answerable for
this fact — in a context neither form and no mission uses:

> You want to know when the last Sunday bus leaves. A poster on the shelter says
> 6:40. The bus company's own timetable says 7:10.
> **The bus company's timetable, because they run the buses.**

The wrong answers now mirror post-6's exactly: topic-relevance (*"the poster,
because it is about this exact stop"*) and cynicism (*"neither, you can never
really be sure"*).

### The pairs asked for different things

Two of the three verification pairs measured different task demands across
windows, so a difference between them could be a property of the items rather
than of the child.

**Pre-4** asked *"How much should that sentence make you believe it?"* — rate
your belief, and recognise a slogan. **Post-4** asked *"What should you do
next?"* — choose an action. Pre-4 now asks for an action, with the same three
distractor kinds post-4 uses: confidence-as-evidence, ask-it-again, and
check-a-source as the correct move.

**Pre-5** asked for the best *question*; post-5 asked for the best *thing to
do*. Pre-5 now asks for an action, mirroring post-5's structure: an inspection
distractor, a spreading distractor, and provenance as the correct answer.

### The framing was not earned

The module claimed the forms are *"parallel by skill and difficulty"* and that
the post window *"measures transfer"*. Neither is established. Nothing has been
piloted. Nothing has been equated. **Each skill carries exactly one item**, so a
single response moves a whole competency by 33.3 points.

I cannot fix that by writing more carefully — it needs several reviewed items
per skill and a pilot, which is not something this sprint can produce. So the
honest fix is the narrow label, applied everywhere at once:

- The module doc now states what the instrument *is not*, at length: not
  equated, one item per skill, matched only by authorship and review, "which is
  the weakest kind of parallelism there is".
- `growthPoints` is renamed **`pointsDifference`** on the domain object, with a
  comment saying why: *"Naming the field for the statistic it is not is how the
  overclaim gets back in."*
- Every human-facing label follows — the admin dashboard, the benchmark page,
  the school report, the CSV column, the teacher class page, the marketing
  copy, the retention note.
- The public benchmark page gains a paragraph stating the limit outright, in the
  section already called "How to read the result honestly", ending: *"We would
  rather say that than sell you a word the instrument has not earned."*

## Finding 2 — the child was told an incomplete truth

The preamble said: *"Your teacher only uses this to decide what the class should
practise."*

Cohort results reach school leaders through annual reports, the certification
dashboard and the CSV export. Aggregated, suppressed, and still not "your
teacher only". Told to a seven-year-old, that is a promise about who sees their
work, and it was not accurate.

Both forms now say:

> *"Nobody gets a score, and nobody sees your answers."*
> *"Adults at your school only see results for whole groups, to help them decide
> what a class should practise next."*

True, child-sized, and it says the part that matters most to a child — nobody
sees *your* answers — before the part about groups. The post form's outro moved
from "your teacher will look at" to "adults will look at", for the same reason.

## Guards added

Five, and two of them are the point:

- **No benchmark item may treat an inscription, an object's age, or apparent
  officialness as source authority**, and no correct option may rest on
  proximity. This is the mission-side rule, now enforced outside missions, which
  is where it escaped to.
- **Every pre item's post pair must ask for the same kind of move** — same stem
  shape, same option count, reading load within twelve words. This is what
  would have caught pre-4 and pre-5.
- Both source items must make the responsible source the correct answer.
- One item per skill per form is asserted, so the constraint the honesty rests
  on is visible in the tests rather than only in a comment.
- Both intros must say nobody sees your answers and that adults see group
  results, and must **not** say "your teacher only uses this".

**348 tests, up from 343.**

## What this says about the review process

Sprint 18 fixed a rationale in a mission and I did not check whether the same
rationale lived anywhere else. It did — in the instrument that measures whether
children learned the lesson. **A content fix has to be swept across every place
that content's logic appears**, and for eleven sprints I treated the missions as
the whole of the content. The benchmark is content too, and it had never been
read against the checklist the missions were being held to.

That is now three sprints running where the defect was in the same shape: sprint
22 and 23 in the reporting layer, this one in the assessment layer, all of them
outside the missions and all of them saying something the missions had already
been corrected not to say. The missions got twenty-one sprints of attention. The
things that report on them got none until somebody looked.

The second finding is smaller and worth naming separately: a privacy statement
addressed to a child is still a privacy statement. It was the only one in the
product that had never been checked against what the product actually does.

## Deliberately left

- **The instrument still cannot support growth or transfer claims**, and no
  amount of copywriting changes that. What it needs is multiple reviewed items
  per skill and a pilot that equates the forms. The labels are now honest about
  that; the instrument is unchanged.
- **The certification content has never been read against the checklist**, and
  it is the last body of authored content that has not.
- Every mission has been read once, none twice.
