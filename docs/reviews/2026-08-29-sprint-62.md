# Sprint 62 — a provenance claim the product could not support, and a real flake

**Date:** 2026-08-29
**Scope:** two narrow corrections. The academic-settings note, and the
documented check-in retry flake. No product broadening.

## 1. The form told administrators where the data came from

Sprint 60 stopped `AcademicDatesForm` prefilling values the product would
reject, passing `null` instead. The form then inferred:

```ts
const missing = !startsOn || !endsOn;
```

So a **corrupted current record** produced *"This school year has no recorded
dates"* and an explanation about *"records brought forward from an earlier
version"*. The parent panel simultaneously said the year and dates **need
configuring** — two different accounts of the same state, one screen apart.

That is a claim about **provenance**, and the product has no basis for it. A
district administrator cannot tell an absent legacy column from a record
somebody corrupted last week, and the two call for different responses: one is a
migration to finish, the other is a mistake to correct.

### The correction

`academicSettingsState(school)` decides from **what is stored**, not from
whether the prefills came back null:

- **`absent`** — the genuine migration-era shape and nothing else: both dates
  empty, and the label either empty too or a real school year. A database from
  before sprint 32 had nowhere to put these.
- **`unreadable`** — anything present and unusable: either date, the label, or a
  combination that does not hold together. **Mixed states count here**: one date
  absent and one malformed is not a clean migration.
- **`ok`** — no note at all.

The unreadable copy makes no provenance claim and **echoes nothing**:

> **This school year's settings cannot be read.** The school year or one of its
> dates is not in a form this product can use, so rollover and automatic
> deletion are paused. Nothing has been deleted and no class or student record
> has changed. Enter the year and both dates below to correct it.

Naming the stored value back is how a wrong record gets copied into its own
correction, so the note does not — asserted by a test that forbids any date-like
string in it.

## 2. The check-in retry flake was the test, and the product is right

`tests/checkin-player.test.tsx` → *"offers Try again, and advances once the retry
succeeds"* asserted `Story 2` **synchronously** after clicking Retry. But
`confirm` advances inside an async `startTransition`, so the save resolves on a
microtask that can land after `user.click` has finished awaiting `act()`.

**I checked the product first**, because a flake here could have been a real
double-submit or a stale index:

- `if (!selected || saving) return;` — the transition's own pending flag guards
  a second submit while one is in flight.
- `optionId` and `itemId` are captured **before** the transition opens.
- `index` and `isLast` come from the closure at click time, which is the correct
  submission's values, not whatever a later render holds.

No defect. The assertion is what was wrong.

### The correction

`findByRole` instead of `getByRole` — it waits for the state the successful
transition produces. **No sleep and no retry of the action**: if the child never
advances, it fails.

Strengthened while there, so the wait cannot hide anything:

- the child is asserted to still be on **Story 1** before Retry is clicked;
- `submitCheckInAnswer` is called **exactly twice in total**;
- **both** calls asserted with `toHaveBeenNthCalledWith` and the same option, so
  the retry resubmits the child's original answer and nothing else is smuggled
  in by the transition.

**Proof it still bites**: with `setIndex(index + 1)` temporarily removed from
the player, the test fails — after waiting out the timeout — along with two
others. The product file was restored immediately and `git diff` confirms it
clean.

## A correction to the record

Sprints 60 and 61 both described this flake as being *"in the mission player"*.
It is not, and never was: the test exists only in
`tests/checkin-player.test.tsx`. I wrote the attribution from memory of a
truncated failure line rather than from the file, and then repeated it. Both
review records are corrected in place.

## The lesson

**A UI that infers cause from a sanitised value is guessing.** Sprint 60 made the
form safer by refusing to prefill bad data — and in doing so destroyed the only
evidence the form had, because `null` then meant both "never recorded" and "we
would not repeat that back to you". The fix that removed the danger also removed
the distinction, and nothing failed, because the sentence it produced was still
grammatical.

When a value is deliberately narrowed on its way to a component, ask what the
component was inferring from the width it used to have.

# Sprint 63 — the correction: the new copy overstated what was blocked

Independent review withheld acceptance on the wording I had just written, and it
is a material privacy claim rather than a phrasing quibble.

## The defect

The unreadable note said *"rollover and automatic deletion are paused"* and
*"Nothing has been deleted and no class or student record has changed."* The
Program page repeated it as *"before rollover and retention can work"* and
*"Retention and rollover are blocked."*

Both overstate, and the implemented model is narrower than either:

- **Retention is per cohort.** A class carries its own snapshotted
  `year_ends_on`, and `runScheduledPurge` **deliberately keeps deleting**
  cohorts whose own date is valid and past. That partial-purge behaviour is
  exactly what sprint 60 built, and I then described it as stopped.
- **What an unreadable school calendar actually blocks** is the rollover
  preview and the current-year summary date, plus any cohort with no usable date
  of its own.
- **"Nothing has been deleted" is a claim about history the product cannot
  make.** A purge may well have run last week.

An administrator reading that could believe all scheduled deletion had stopped,
or that no prior purge had occurred. On a retention control, that is the wrong
thing to be wrong about.

## The correction

One shared `RETENTION_SCOPE` sentence, used by both notes so they cannot drift:

> Rollover and this year's retention date cannot be worked out until it is
> corrected. Classes that already have a valid recorded year-end still follow
> their own deletion dates, and any class without one is not deleted
> automatically. Viewing or correcting this page does not itself delete any
> student work.

The migration note carries it too — it previously ended *"Nothing is deleted
automatically until you fill these in"*, the same overstatement in the other
state. The Program summary and the Academic year panel now say rollover and
**this year's retention date** cannot be worked out, and the panel adds that
classes with a valid recorded year-end keep their own deletion dates.

The last clause is deliberately present tense. The product can say what looking
at this page does; it cannot say what has already happened.

### Deliberately unchanged

Three other places say retention is blocked school-wide, and they are **correct**:
the Data page's policy hint and per-row `unrecognised-policy` reason, and the
report's retention line. Those describe an unrecognised **retention window**,
which sprint 54 made skip the whole school.

**This paragraph was wrong about a fourth.** I also left the Data page's
`purgeDateFor(school) === null` hint alone as though it belonged to that set. It
does not: that branch is the academic **date** case, and it carried the same
false global claim — *"Set the academic dates on Program & plan. Nothing is
deleted until you do."* Corrected in sprint 64 below.

## Coverage

Three cases added, **all three failing against the sprint-62 copy**: a shared
`it.each` over both states asserting the four accurate clauses and forbidding
five overbroad phrasings by regex — *"nothing has been deleted"*, *"no class or
student record has changed"*, *"automatic deletion is/are paused"*, *"nothing is
deleted automatically until"*, *"retention is/are blocked/paused"* — plus a guard
that the Program page does not reintroduce either of its two.

**667 tests, up from 664.**

## Browser

Program & plan at **1280×800** and **768×1024**, both states:

| surface | reads |
|---|---|
| page summary | *"…before rollover and this year's retention date can be worked out."* |
| Academic year panel | *"…cannot be worked out until the year and both dates are set below. Classes with a valid recorded year-end keep their own deletion dates."* |

All five overbroad phrasings absent from the rendered page in both states; no

## The lesson

**I wrote reassurance instead of a description.** The sentence I reached for —
"nothing has been deleted, nothing has changed" — is what you say to calm
somebody down, and it was making two factual claims: one about the past I could
not check, and one about scope that contradicted a mechanism I had built three
sprints earlier specifically so that valid cohorts *would* keep being deleted.

Comfort and accuracy pull in opposite directions on a broken-state screen, and
the comforting version is the one that writes itself. On a privacy control, the
description has to win.

# Sprint 64 — the fourth branch, which I had recorded as correct

Independent review found the same false global claim one branch further on, in
the page I had just audited.

## The defect

`src/app/admin/data/page.tsx` has two blocking branches for the *"This year
due"* figure, and they are not the same kind of block:

- **`policyBlocked`** — an unrecognised retention **window**. Sprint 54 makes
  `runScheduledPurge` skip the whole school, so *"Nothing is deleted
  automatically"* is true.
- **`purgeDateFor(school) === null`** — an unusable academic **date**. This costs
  the school-level summary figure and nothing else: every cohort still carries
  its own snapshotted year-end, and the purge keeps acting on the valid ones.

The second branch said *"Set the academic dates on Program & plan. Nothing is
deleted until you do."* — the exact claim sprint 63 had removed from the Program
page, sitting on the page whose other branches I had checked and declared
correct in the same commit.

The contradiction was visible on one screen: the summary said nothing would be
deleted while three rows underneath showed **June 12, 2027** as their deletion
date.

## The correction

Only that branch:

> This year's date is unavailable until the academic dates are set on Program &
> plan. Classes with a valid recorded year-end still follow their own deletion
> dates, and any class without one is not deleted automatically.

The `policyBlocked` branch is untouched and still says nothing is deleted
automatically, because for that one it is true. Nothing in the subscription copy
was touched.

## Coverage

Two cases, one failing against the previous page. The first asserts the academic
branch carries the scoped wording, **forbids the old sentence by exact text**,
and asserts the policy branch still carries its deliberately global one — so the
two cannot be confused again or "fixed" together. The second builds a school with
an unreadable year-end and two cohorts, and proves the model the copy now
describes: the summary has no date, the valid cohort still has one, and the
malformed cohort is blocked.

**669 tests, up from 667.**

## Browser

Data & retention at **1280×800** and **768×1024**, school year-end
`"2026-13-45"`, three valid cohorts and one malformed:

- **Summary**: *"Not set"* with the scoped hint.
- **Rows**: three showing **June 12, 2027**, one *"Blocked"* with the
  cohort-level explanation.
- Summary and rows now **agree**; the old sentence is absent; no raw malformed
  value appears anywhere; no overflow.
- With `retention_months = -12` instead, the policy branch still reads *"Blocked
  — Nothing is deleted automatically while the retention window needs
  configuration."*

Demo restored: 2025-2026 / 2025-08-25 / 2026-06-12, retention 12, four cohorts on
2026-06-12, 90 students, 884 attempts, 6 audit rows.

## The lesson

**I audited the file and cleared it by category rather than by branch.** Sprint
63 asked "which of these say retention is globally blocked, and is that true?",
found three that were about the retention window, and wrote them off as a set —
without noticing that a fourth string on the same page belonged to a different
condition entirely. Grouping by what the sentences *said* hid the fact that they
answered to different `if`s.

When copy is being checked against a mechanism, the unit is the branch, not the
page and not the phrasing.
