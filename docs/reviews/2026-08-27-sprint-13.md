## Finding 1 — "The Class Photo" grounded consent in deletability

The mission drew the line between a school website and a personal share
**technically**: the school "can take a page down", and "nobody can take back a
picture that has been sent to a cousin."

That is not true, and it is not the reason. A public web page can be downloaded,
screenshotted, cached and reshared; taking the page down removes the school's
copy and nothing else. A child who learns the deletability rule has learned
something that will fail them the first time they meet a screenshot.

**The line is now consent, purpose, audience and accountable process.** Twenty-
two people said yes to a page on the school website. Nobody asked them about a
cousin. A yes is for a particular place and a particular audience, and it does
not stretch. A second full-credit route records escalation: tell Theo to ask
Ms. Okafor, because she is the one who asked everybody in the first place and
she is answerable for where it goes.

The wrap-up now states the limit honestly rather than relying on it: *a school
can take its own page down; it cannot gather up copies people already saved.*

### The worse half of this finding

The old scene had a second full-credit answer — ask whether Ravi is in the photo
Theo is sending — and it resolved with Theo obtaining the Ravi-free version and
sending that. The mission taught, in its own resolution, that **removing the one
objector manufactures permission from the twenty-two who were never asked.**

That option is now `partial`: a good instinct and an incomplete answer. "Ravi is
not the only one who agreed to a school page and nothing else. Take him out and
there are still twenty-two people who were never asked about a cousin." The
ending resolves through the school instead — Ms. Okafor says the photo is for
the school page, and the cousin is welcome to look at the page like anybody
else. Purpose-limited, and the cousin still sees it.

A misconception entry carries the exact sentence a class will say: *"Ravi is not
in this one, so I can send it."*

## Finding 2 — "The Book That Was Not There" proved nonexistence from one catalogue

A tool invents a book. Mr. Ruiz searches the district catalogue — four hundred
thousand books — and finds nothing. The mission concluded, for full credit,
**"The book does not exist. It was made up."**

Four hundred thousand books is one library district. A real book that is new,
self-published, digital, out of print, or simply never bought locally returns
exactly the same empty result. The mission then generalised the error into
rules: "real books have shelves", "if it is real, you can hold it", and a family
summary claiming *no library anywhere* had it.

This is the verification error the mission exists to prevent, taught by the
mission. Absence of local evidence became evidence of global absence.

**The conclusion is now staged.** From the district search a child gets two
full-credit answers: *"I cannot find it, so I cannot use it yet"* — which is
exactly what they needed to decide — and *"This is one library district. We need
somewhere bigger to look."* Declaring it invented is now a **retry**, with a
coach note saying so plainly: this is the productive wrong answer, most classes
will pick it, and it is a conclusion they have not earned yet.

A new scene supplies the independent check. Mr. Ruiz searches libraries all over
the world, then the publisher records "where every book gets its own number when
it is printed." Nothing on paper, nothing as an ebook, nothing as an audiobook.
*Now* nobody wrote this book.

The rule the child leaves with is a record, not a shelf: *"Not every real book
is on your library shelf. Some are new, some are ebooks, some are audiobooks.
Every one of them leaves a record somewhere, and finding that record is the part
that makes it yours to use."* The badge is Record Checker.

The extension was the same error as an activity: three titles, two real, check
the catalogue. A real book the library happens not to stock would have looked
exactly like the invented one, and the pair would have "confirmed" a false
positive. The two real titles must now be **looked up and confirmed in your
catalogue beforehand**, and the debrief asks what the empty result proved — that
they cannot use it, not that nobody wrote it.

## Guards added

Seven tests, plus three phrases added to the brittle-absolutes ban list so they
are refused across the whole curriculum: `nobody can take back a picture`,
`real books have shelves`, `when nothing anywhere has heard of it`.

- The photo decision's strong answers must reason from agreement and audience,
  must include an escalation route, and must not claim a page can be recalled.
- The check-Ravi option must be `partial`/`developing` and must name the other
  twenty-two, and the guide must carry the "Ravi is not in this one"
  misconception.
- The wrap-up must state both the scope rule and the limit of removal.
- The book's district scene must offer "cannot use it yet" and "somewhere
  bigger" as the strong answers, and "it was made up" must be a retry.
- The wider-check scene must name world libraries, publisher records, ebooks and
  audiobooks before concluding.
- The rule must be a record rule, and "if a book is real, you can hold it" must
  be gone.
- The extension must use preverified titles and must say what a miss proves.

249 tests, up from 242.

## What this says about the review process

Six missions read, six with findings, and the rate has not moved after two
sprints of fixes. Fourteen of the twenty-seven have still never been read by a
person.

The new thing this sprint is that both defects were **structural**, not
phrasing. In each case the wrong reason was wired into which choice scored
`demonstrated`, and in the photo mission the ending itself acted the error out.
Sprint 11 and 12 could be fixed by rewriting sentences. These needed the
decision tree changed — a retry promoted, a full-credit answer demoted, a scene
inserted.

That matters for estimating the remaining fourteen: the cost of a finding is not
uniformly "edit some copy."

## Deliberately left

- **Fourteen Sprint 09 missions unread.** Unchanged and now the dominant risk.
- **The eight legacy forced-award scenes**, still untraced against the coaching

- No general guard against factual error exists or can.
