# Sprint 25 review — 27 August 2026

**Scope.** The educator certification — the last body of authored content that
had never been read against the checklist. Both findings correct. As predicted
in sprint 24's handoff, it repeated something the missions had already been
corrected not to say.

---

## Finding 1 — the product certified five button clicks

`completeCertificationAction` checks that every module has *some* saved answer.
That is all. A teacher can pick the wrong option in all five checks and unlock
the artefact.

That is not a bug. The teacher page says so out loud — *"the check after each
module is not a gate and there is no pass mark"* — and for adult professional
learning inside a low-stakes classroom tool it is a defensible design. The
printed document was careful too: **Certificate of completion**.

The defect was everything between those two honest ends. The admin dashboard
said **"Educators certified"**. The staff page said **"Certified"**. The plans
page sold an **"educator micro-certification"**. A principal reading "3 of 4
certified" reasonably concludes their staff understood the privacy, verification
and classroom-use material. The system knows five buttons were clicked.

### The model I chose, and why

The finding offered two honest models. I took (a) — keep the ungated checks and
rename the offering — rather than (b), gating on correctness.

The reason is that the non-gating is a deliberate product decision with a
rationale already written into the UI, and turning adult professional learning
into a quiz that withholds a certificate is a real change to what the product
is. That is the school's call and the author's, not something a review finding
should trigger from me. Renaming preserves the design and removes the
overclaim; gating would preserve the word and change the design.

**It is now an orientation, everywhere.** Admin dashboard, staff page and its
table column, program checklist, school report, plans, approach and for-schools
pages, teacher nav and teacher home, the module content, the audit detail, the
seed comment, and the type doc. The metric is **"Orientation completed"**, with
the dashboard hint saying what it means: *"5 modules read and answered. Not a
competence check."*

Two surfaces now state the limit rather than implying it:

- The teacher page, extending the sentence that was already there: *"That is
  also why this is called an orientation rather than a certification: finishing
  it records that you read the modules and answered the checks, and your school
  sees exactly that. It does not claim you got them right, because nobody is
  checking."*
- **The certificate itself**, which a teacher may hand to a principal: *"This
  records completion of the orientation. The check after each module is not
  gated, so this is not an assessment of understanding and does not certify
  competence in any of the areas listed."*

## Finding 2 — module 1 gave teachers a false inventory

> *"So the curriculum is nine situations, not nine rules."*

The product has **27 missions, three per skill**. Sprint 09 tripled it and
sprint 10 interleaved it specifically so a child meets each decision three times
in different contexts. The one document that tells a teacher what they are
teaching still described the version from before that work — so a teacher would
plan nine one-off stories and never learn that the repetition is the method.

It now says: *"The curriculum is 27 authored missions, three for each of the
nine skills, so a child meets the same decision in three different situations
rather than once,"* with a key point carrying the same fact.

### And an overclaimed rationale

> *"Children at this age are concrete thinkers, and they learn decision rules
> far better from specific situations than from principles."*
> *"Concrete situations transfer better than stated rules at this age."*

A universal about every seven-to-ten-year-old and a causal claim about transfer,
neither of which this program has evidence for — and both of which flatten the
children who reason perfectly well in the abstract. It is also the same category
of error the missions were corrected for repeatedly: a statement about what
children *are*, doing work that a statement about what to *do* should be doing.

Replaced with the actual method, framed as a sequence:

> *"The shape of each encounter is deliberate: a specific situation first, then
> the decision rule named out loud, then the same rule met again somewhere new.
> That is a sequence to teach with, not a claim about what a seven year old is
> capable of. Plenty of children this age reason perfectly well in the abstract,
> and the repetition is there so they get practice applying a rule rather than
> because they cannot hold one."*

The third paragraph carried a smaller version of the same thing — *"what they
cannot yet do reliably is generalise from an abstract warning"* — and now says
that an abstract warning is thin material at any age, and thinner still about a
situation nobody has met.

## Guards added

Six. Two are sweeps and one is a behavioural assertion worth keeping:

- **The offering's own copy may not contain "certified" or "certification"**,
  and `CERTIFICATION_TITLE` may not either.
- Module 1 must state 27 missions and three per skill, and must not say "the
  curriculum is nine situations".
- No module may say children at this age *are* concrete thinkers, or use "far
  better", or claim concrete situations transfer better than rules.
- **A teacher answering every check wrongly still completes.** This asserts the
  design rather than complaining about it, and it is the test that keeps the
  naming honest: as long as completion does not validate correctness, nothing
  may report it as certification. If somebody later gates the checks, this test
  fails and the naming decision gets revisited deliberately.
- The completion record must carry no score, pass, correct, grade or result
  field — so the word cannot quietly change back on the strength of a field
  nobody discussed.

## What this says about the review process

Sprint 24's handoff said: *"the certification content has never been read
against the checklist, and it is the last body of authored content that has not.
Given sprint 24, assume it repeats something the missions have already been
corrected not to say."* It did — the developmental universal, which is the
fourth time some version of "children of this age are X" has had to come out.

The count error is the more instructive one though. Sprint 09 tripled the
curriculum and sprint 10 rewrote how it is ordered, and **neither sprint touched
the document that explains the curriculum to teachers**. Eleven sprints of
content review went past it. A change to the shape of a product has to sweep the
things that describe the product, and the review process here had no step that
did that.

Every body of authored content in this repository has now been read at least
once. That is missions, benchmark and orientation. The pattern across the last
four sprints is consistent enough to state plainly: **the missions were reviewed
to death and everything that surrounds them was not** — the roll-up that reports
on them, the instrument that assesses them, and the document that explains them
each carried a defect the missions themselves had already been fixed for.

## Deliberately left

- **The orientation is ungated by design and stays that way.** If the product
  should certify competence, that is a decision to make deliberately: gate the
  checks, allow unpenalised retries, give misconception-specific feedback, and
  validate correctness in the completion action. The tests are written so that
  choice reopens the naming question rather than sliding past it.
- **Nothing verifies marketing copy against product behaviour.** The plans and
  for-schools pages describe the product in prose that no test reads.
- Every mission has been read once, none twice.
