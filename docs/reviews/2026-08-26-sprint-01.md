# Sprint 01 review — 26 August 2026

**Scope.** The initial MVP: nine missions, the student player and check-in,
teacher dashboards and discussion guides, the educator micro-certification,
administrator dashboards, retention controls and the annual school report.

**Method.** Ran against the checklist in `../classroom-review.md`. Group review
on projector-sized viewports (1280×800 and 1366×768). Independent review at
890×762 and 768×1024, with every interactive control measured rather than
eyeballed. Content read aloud in full. Schema inspected directly for new
fields. Findings 9 to 11 came from a second review pass after Classroom Mode
landed.

---

## Findings and what changed

### 1. No teacher-led facilitation experience at all — fixed

The largest gap. Every teacher surface assumed students on devices, but the
realistic first lesson in grades 2–4 is the whole class on one board, and
"nine missions to assign" is not a lesson.

**Added Classroom Mode** (`/teacher/classroom/[slug]`): a plan screen with the
decision points and what to watch for, a projector view with type scaled by
`clamp()`, teacher-revealable branches, a hands-up tally, hideable coach notes,
arrow-key driving, and a paced debrief. It records nothing.

The branch reveal is the part that matters instructionally: in a group the best
question is "what would have happened if we picked B?", and until now the only
way to answer it was to have a child choose badly on purpose.

### 2. Fresh missions skipped their opening story scenes — fixed

`resumeSceneId` advanced past leading story scenes unconditionally, so a child
opening a mission for the first time landed on the first decision with no
narrative context. Now only a genuine resume skips forward.

### 3. Student navigation targets were ~36px — fixed

The map, badges, avatar chip, log out, save-and-exit and read-aloud controls
were all below the 44px minimum. All raised to `min-h-11`. The big obvious
buttons had always been fine; the navigation had not been measured.

### 4. The check-in had the densest text and no read-aloud — fixed

Read-aloud shipped only in the mission player. The benchmark scenarios are
longer and less supported than mission narration, are met without a teacher
beside the child, and are the one place a decoding failure silently becomes a
measurement error. Read-aloud added to the check-in intro, every item and the
closing screen.

### 5. Classroom Mode hid its illustration below 1024px — fixed

Found during the group review. A board mirrored from a laptop is often narrower
than a desktop; the illustration disappeared and left the story floating in an
empty panel. Stretching it into a banner cropped it to an unreadable strip. Now
a proportional column from `sm` upward.

### 6. Unassigning a mission reported "0 of 23 finished" — fixed

Completion was read from a structure keyed on currently-assigned missions, so
removing a mission from a class made the work students had already done appear
to vanish. Counted across all missions now.

### 7. Archiving a class could shorten retention below school policy — fixed

Retention was anchored to `archived_at`. Archiving in March would have deleted
records months before the school-wide schedule said they would go, and
archiving in September would have pushed them past it. Anchored to the end of
the school year only; archiving is now purely organisational.

### 8. Seeded benchmark growth was +51 points — fixed

Not a bug, a credibility problem. The first thing an instructional-technology
director would disbelieve is a fifty-point jump. Retuned to 47% → 71%.

### 9. Classroom Mode buried its launch control below the fold — fixed

Raised in a second review pass. The plan screen put "Put it on the board" at
the bottom, after the setup notes, decision points, what-to-watch-for and the
keyboard reference. At 1366×768 — a very ordinary classroom laptop — that is
below the fold, so a teacher had to scroll to start a lesson with a class
already sitting on the rug.

The launch control now sits in the header and stays there in a sticky strip
while the plan is scrolled. The bottom action is kept for a teacher who has
read to the end.

### 10. The check-in saved and advanced on the first tap — fixed

The most consequential finding of the sprint. Choosing an option immediately
wrote the answer and moved to the next story. For seven to ten year olds on
Chromebook trackpads that is an accidental-answer machine, and the cost is not
a moment of annoyance: it is a wrong measurement that nobody can detect later,
in the one part of the product whose entire purpose is measuring accurately.

Answering is now two deliberate steps. The first tap only selects, with a
strong visible state — denim border, filled background, and the number badge
replaced by a check mark. An explicit **Next** saves it and advances, and is
disabled until something is chosen. **Back** discards anything unconfirmed and
restores the saved answer, so revisiting a story never silently rewrites it.

Supporting changes:

- Options became native radios inside labels, so arrow keys move the selection
  and the browser manages the roving tab stop. Selecting still submits nothing.
- A status line states the rule in the child's own terms: *"Nothing is saved
  until you tap Next."*
- The visible "Story 3 of 9" counter is now `aria-hidden`, because the sr-only
  heading beside it already announced the same thing and screen reader users
  were hearing it twice.
- Twelve tests added in `tests/checkin-player.test.tsx` covering
  accidental-tap protection, the disabled-until-selected rule, changing a mind
  before confirming, forward and back navigation, resume, discarding
  unconfirmed edits, keyboard operation and the absence of any score.

### 11. The student header home link was 32px — fixed

Found while measuring every control at 768×1024 after the check-in rework. The
logo link in the student header had escaped the earlier touch-target pass.
Every interactive control on a student surface now measures at least 44px, with
the sole exception of the visually hidden skip link, which is correct.

## Checked and found acceptable

- **Calibrated trust, not avoidance.** Every verification mission ends at a
  source rather than a shrug, and certification module 3 names universal
  cynicism as a failure of the unit. Four Doors explicitly refuses "never use
  the tool" as an answer.
- **Judgement over tool operation.** No mission teaches where a button is. The
  transferable artefacts are phrases: *sounding sure is not the same as being
  right*, *good help leaves the thinking with you*, *ask who would actually
  know*.
- **No addictive mechanics.** No streaks, timers, points, leaderboards or loss
  framing anywhere. Badges are flat, all nine equal, and the badge page says so.
- **Calm feedback.** Unsafe choices loop back with an explanation; a structural
  test enforces that every `rethink` branch offers another go and records no
  evidence.
- **Touch targets.** Every control on a student surface measured at 768×1024
  and 1366×768; all at least 44px. Check-in options are 68px.
- **Cultural inclusion.** Guidance says "a grown-up you trust" throughout; the
  only two uses of "parents" are in the mouths of the manipulative in-story
  systems, which is how those messages actually read. Roster names span a wide
  range. Nothing assumes a bedroom, a car or a device at home.
- **Privacy.** This sprint added no student-facing field. Classroom Mode adds
  no table and writes nothing. The hands-up tally is component state that is
  cleared on every scene change and never leaves the browser.
- **Teacher workload.** Two clicks from the mission library to a lesson on the
  board.

---

## Deliberately left

- **No separate presenter display.** A second screen showing notes while the
  board shows the story would be better, but most classrooms mirror rather than
  extend. The toggleable notes strip is the honest fit; revisit if presenter
  displays turn out to be common.
- **Read-aloud uses the browser voice.** Quality varies by platform and it will
  mispronounce some names. A recorded narration track per mission is the right
  answer and is a content production job, not an engineering one.
- **Classroom Mode has no student-device pairing.** Deliberate: the whole point
  is that it needs no devices. Revisit only if teachers ask.
