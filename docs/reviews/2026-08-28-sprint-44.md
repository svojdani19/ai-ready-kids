# Sprint 44 — the claim that lived in the chrome

**Date:** 2026-08-28
**Scope:** one buyer-facing copy defect and the guard that should have caught it.

## The finding

The shared site header told school buyers that teachers can **"Preview, assign,
discuss, certify"**.

Sprint 25 renamed those five modules an *educator orientation* for a specific
reason: **the checks are ungated**. A teacher can answer every one of them
incorrectly and still receive a certificate of completion. The only fact the
system holds is that five modules were opened and five questions answered.

So "certify" told a principal something the data cannot support. It also
contradicted three surfaces the product had already corrected — the approach
page ("finishing it records completion rather than certifying competence"), the
annual report ("the checks are not gated, so it is not evidence of competence")
and the certificate itself ("does not certify"). The navigation was the one
place still making the claim, and navigation is what a buyer reads first.

## Why the guard missed it

The existing regression test scanned `CERTIFICATION_MODULES` and
`CERTIFICATION_TITLE` — **the offering's own content constants**. The claim was
not in the offering. It was in `src/components/SiteHeader.tsx`, a shared
component whose nav blurbs are marketing copy that no content test had reason to
open. A guard scoped to a content module cannot see a claim made in chrome.

Worth noting: the blurbs render **only at desktop width**. The mobile menu shows
labels without them, so the sentence was invisible at 768px and below — one more
reason it survived review.

## The commercial rationale

Certification is a word schools buy on. A district that believes its staff are
*certified* has been told something about risk and compliance posture, and it
will repeat that to a board, a parent, or an insurer. When the underlying data is
"five pages were opened", the gap surfaces at exactly the wrong moment — an
incident, an audit, a renewal.

This product's honest position is stronger than the overclaim and it already
exists elsewhere in the copy: a short, practical orientation that respects
teachers' time, with a certificate of completion that says what it is. Selling
that is defensible for as long as the contract lasts. Selling "certify" is
defensible until somebody asks what the certificate means.

The narrow fix is also the right one: **the answer is not to gate the checks.**
Ungated checks are a defensible design for adult professional learning, and
gating them to justify a word would be letting marketing copy drive pedagogy.
The copy changes to match the product.

## What changed

**One blurb.** "Preview, assign, discuss, certify" → **"Preview, assign, discuss,
prepare"**. It matches the destination section, which offers preview, Classroom
Mode, evidence, guides and *"a 5-module educator orientation… with a printable
certificate of completion"*.

**A sweep across every buyer-facing surface** found nothing else. The remaining
uses in marketing copy are all accurate and were deliberately kept:

- *"certificate of completion"* — what a teacher actually receives;
- *"does not claim any compliance certification"* (privacy), *"not a legal
  compliance certification"* (report), *"not a compliance certification"*
  (data) — explicit denials, which a sweep must not tidy away;
- *"rather than certifying competence"* (approach) and *"does not certify"*
  (certificate) — disclaimers that need the word to do their job.

## The new guard

Regression coverage now reads **the marketing surfaces a buyer actually looks
at**: the header, the footer, and every page under `(site)`. For each file it
strips comments, imports and code identifiers, collapses whitespace, removes the
allowed accurate phrases, and fails on any `certif` left standing.

Three details that make it a guard rather than a formality:

- **Whitespace is collapsed before scanning**, because prose wraps. The privacy
  page's "does not claim any compliance certification" spans two source lines,
  and a line-by-line scan read the tail as a bare claim.
- **Comments and imports are stripped**, so a note explaining why a claim was
  removed does not read as the claim returning. My own explanatory comment
  tripped the first draft.
- **The accurate phrases are asserted positively too.** A separate test requires
  "certificate of completion" and "educator orientation" on the for-schools page
  and the compliance denial on the privacy page, so a future sweep cannot pass by
  deleting honest copy.

Plus a test on the blurb itself: no `certif`, still mentions preview and assign,
and says prepare or orient.

Confirmed to **fail against the old header** by stashing it: both the file scan
and the blurb test go red. **513 tests, up from 501.**

## Verified in the browser

**1280×800.** Menu opens on the trigger, `aria-expanded` toggles true, four items
render with blurbs, the Teachers line reads "Preview, assign, discuss, prepare",
no overflow, nothing clipped. All four links are tabbable and visible, they carry
`focus-visible` styling, and Escape closes the menu.

**768×1024.** The mobile menu renders labels without blurbs, grouped under "The
program" and "For your school". No horizontal overflow, nothing clipped, labels
legible.

## Observed, not fixed

**Escape leaves focus on a hidden element.** With the desktop menu open and a
link focused, pressing Escape closes the menu but focus stays on that link, which
is still in the DOM and no longer visible. A keyboard user pressing Escape then
Tab resumes from somewhere they cannot see. Focus should return to the trigger.
This is pre-existing and unrelated to the copy defect, so it is reported rather
than folded into a copy-only sprint.

## Out of scope, as instructed

- **The checks are not gated and the orientation is not broadened.**
- **Lapsed-subscription enforcement stays documented and unbuilt.** It needs a
  lifecycle decision and an action-inventory audit, not a copy patch.
- Child-data and authored-curriculum constraints untouched; nothing in this
  sprint reaches student records.

## The lesson

**A guard scoped to a feature cannot see a claim made in the chrome around it.**
The product corrected this word on the approach page, the report and the
certificate, wrote a test to keep it corrected, and pointed that test at the
content constants — where the word had already been removed. The claim survived
in the one place no test was looking and every buyer looks first.

Shared layout components are copy. When a sprint retires a claim, the sweep has
to cover the navigation, the footer and the marketing pages, not just the feature
that prompted it.
