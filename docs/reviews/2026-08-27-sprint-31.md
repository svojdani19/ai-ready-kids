## Finding 1 — the dashboard invented a history

The student page said, of every skill in the second list:

> *"You worked these out after a Try again."*

`developing` arrives two different ways, and only one of them is that. It is
also recorded **directly by first-go partial choices that continue without any
retry at all** — "Lean over and ask Theo", "Type your school name, but not your
street", "Just close the app and keep it to yourself". A child who made a
thoughtful, partly-safe choice, first time, was told they had needed correcting.

That is a story the data does not support, and it is the kind a seven-year-old
believes about themselves. The whole point of the two-list design was that
falling short should read as partway there rather than as nothing; inventing a
failure the child did not have undoes it.

The empty state was untrue in the same direction: *"Finish a mission and your
first one will appear here."* A child can finish a mission entirely on partial
choices and see nothing at all.

### What changed

The copy describes the **state** and never the history, because the page cannot
tell the two routes apart and should not pretend to:

> **"You made a good start on these. Each one moves up when you get it first go
> in a new story."**

And the empty state is now true whichever way a child got there, pointing at the
thing they *did* earn:

> **"Nothing here yet. These show up when you get one first go. You still get a
> badge for finishing a mission either way."**

The comment above the section now records what the page can and cannot know, so
the sentence does not drift back.

### The part worth noticing

**The teacher's roster legend was already correct**: *"Amber means a partly-right
choice, or the safe answer reached after a Try again."* Both routes, named,
accurate. Only the child was told the invented version.

The audit turned up one more, in the school report: *"reaching the same answer
after a Try again is recorded as developing instead"* — true, and it omitted the
partial route, so an administrator reading it would think amber meant one thing.
Now names both.

## Finding 2 — a teacher who owned a class could not be offboarded

`removeStaffAction` refused while a teacher owned any class and told the
administrator to *"reassign or archive it first"*.

There was no reassign. And `countClassesForTeacher` counted archived classes, so
archiving did not clear it either — the advice was wrong twice over. That left
two ways to remove a departing teacher: **permanently delete every class they
had ever owned**, with each roster, attempt and check-in, or leave the account
live. In a build where staff sign in with a known email address and no password,
leaving it live keeps a former employee's roster access.

A teacher leaving in June is not an edge case.

### What changed

`reassignClass` moves a class to another teacher and keeps everything else —
roster, attempts, check-ins, assignments and **the join code**, so no child has
to be told a new one because an adult left. It carries the same ownership
invariant as `createClass`, in the same place: the target must be a teacher at
that class's own school, so no class can be made ownerless or cross-school by
being moved. Audited with both names.

`reassignClassAction` is administrator-only, and there is a per-row **Reassign**
control on the classes page that excludes the current owner.

The offboarding block now **names** the classes instead of counting them, active
and archived, and gives advice that is true: *"Give them to another teacher on
the Classes page first — archiving does not change who owns a class, and
deleting one takes its roster and records with it."* The staff page lists them
under the count for the same reason. Last-administrator protection is untouched.

## Tests

contains **exactly one** step at that scene — no retry — while the record reads
`developing`. Then copy assertions on all three surfaces, including that the

The offboarding ones cover a move keeping the join code and roster, the old

point of the exercise.

## What this says about the review process

Sprint 20 found that the evidence model reports a child's circumstances as a
skill gap, and I wrote **"nothing checks what a wrong answer costs a child"**
into the handoff as an open item. It stayed there for eleven sprints. It was
never going to be closed by the kind of work those sprints were doing — the
model is correct, the roll-up is correct, the teacher's legend is correct — and
the defect was one sentence on the page a child reads.

**An open item phrased as a general worry does not get closed.** Somebody had to
read the actual sentence against the actual data paths. That is a different
activity from auditing a model, and no amount of the second produces the first.

The second finding is the first workflow defect found here: not something
untrue, not something unsafe, just something a school does every year that the
product made impossible without destroying data. Thirty sprints of review have
asked whether the product is honest and whether it is safe. This is the first
that asked whether it is *usable* on an ordinary Tuesday in June.

## Deliberately left

- **Bulk reassignment.** One class at a time. A teacher with four classes takes
  four moves, each individually confirmed, which is the right default for an
  operation that changes who can see children's names.
- The route and action inventory is still not complete.
