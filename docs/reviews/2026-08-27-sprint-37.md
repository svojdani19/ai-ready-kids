# Sprint 37 — the activity contained the hazard

**Date:** 2026-08-27
**Scope:** one P1 supporting-content finding, in one mission's teacher guide.

## The finding

The extension for **The Filter That Wanted More** read:

> Project three classroom photos you have taken. As a group, list everything in
> each background that could identify the school. Students are usually faster at
> this than adults.

In a mission about what a picture gives away, that put real children's faces,
names on cubbies and on work, uniforms, timetables, accommodation notices and
school signage on a projector, in front of the whole room — and a projected
screen can itself be photographed. It also asked a teacher to source and upload
images, and it sat outside whatever media-consent arrangements the school has
with families.

The lesson meant to teach restraint was normalising the inspection and display
of real children's images. That is the checklist's activity-contains-the-hazard
problem, and whole-class projector use is exactly where it bites hardest.

## What changed

The extension is now **zero-prep and self-supplying**. Three invented photos are
authored inside the product as `guide.extensionCards`, described rather than
shown, so a teacher reads them aloud or puts them on the board and nothing is
sourced, uploaded or photographed. It works with no device at all.

The clues are deliberately of different strength, and the third one is the
point of the activity:

| | Planted clue | Why it is there |
|---|---|---|
| **Photo 1** | A `BRIGHTWOOD FALL FAIR — SATURDAY` banner and a bus route number | A **strong** clue. Proves the banner was behind the photographer — not that either child goes to that school or lives on that route. |
| **Photo 2** | A `CENTRAL LEAGUE U9` shirt | A **weak** clue. Plenty of towns have a Central League. Still a clue once stacked on the others. |
| **Photo 3** | Nothing. A plain wall, no window, no sign | **Clean, and still not ready.** Two children are in it and only one was asked. |

Photo 3 is what keeps the activity from collapsing back into background-spotting.
Without it the room learns that a tidy picture is a safe picture, which is the
exact misconception this mission's own guide already warns teachers to head off:
*"The background was clear, so we could have posted it."* The activity now
carries that lesson instead of undercutting it.

**Every card answers two separate questions** — what does this *suggest*, and
what would we have to know to be *sure* — plus a third about audience. So
"suggests" and "proves" are distinguished on all three cards rather than being
left to one example.

**The room answers by hand signal**, thumb up / flat hand / thumb down, and the
extension says in as many words that nothing is written down about any child.
No tally, no names, nothing recorded.

**It says not to substitute real photographs, and gives the reason:**

> Do not swap in real photos of your class, your students, their families or
> your colleagues — these three exist so that you never have to, and a picture
> on a projector can be photographed off the wall.

A rule with a reason attached survives a busy week; a bare prohibition often
does not.

**Timing.** 411 words of authored material, about three minutes read aloud,
leaving roughly seven for discussion across three cards. The ten-minute framing
holds.

## Implementation

`ExtensionCard` is a new **optional** field on `DiscussionGuide`
(`extensionCards?`), so the other twenty-six missions are untouched and the type
change is additive. It renders in both teacher surfaces — the printable
discussion guide and the mission detail page — and prints with the guide.

## Tests

Eight, all confirmed to **fail against the old extension and pass against the
new** by stashing the content and re-running: no instruction to photograph or
project one's own classroom and nothing in the guide sending a teacher to a
camera roll; three cards present and all fields populated; the readable-aloud
path; the do-not-substitute rule with its reason; suggests-versus-proves on
every card with clues of differing strength; a clean-background card that is
still not approved on audience and permission grounds; hand signals with nothing
recorded; and no real school named.

471 tests, up from 463.

## Verified in the browser

At **1280×800** and **768×1024**, on both the discussion guide and the mission
detail page: no horizontal overflow, all three cards inside the content column,
nothing clipped, 16px body text. Card widths 702px / 662px / 442px respectively.
Screenshots confirmed the rendering at both sizes.

One caveat worth recording: the Browser pane kept going hidden mid-session, and
in that state screenshots return a blank frame for anything below the initially
painted viewport. The geometry above was measured directly from the live layout,
and the screenshots I did capture were taken by hiding the preceding sections so
the extension fell inside the painted region.

## Found but not fixed — same defect, another mission

`the-class-photo`'s extension is the **same defect, unmitigated**, and it names
the sensitive content as the target:

> Take a photo of your own classroom, project it, and have the class hunt the
> background for anything they would not publish. Names on trays, timetables, a
> rota with home details.

This sprint was scoped to one mission, so it is **not changed**. It should be
the next one.

`what-the-camera-sees` is the mitigated case — empty room, before school, never
a live feed, never children in frame, a prepared image offered as an
alternative, and "nothing in this activity turns a camera on in front of the
class." It still asks a teacher to photograph a real school interior, so it is
worth revisiting, but it was clearly authored with the hazard in mind.

Both are now **pinned by a test**: the set of missions whose extension asks for a
real classroom photograph must equal exactly those two. The list can shrink; no
new mission can join it.

## Deliberately not changed

- **The mission graph, evidence, scenes and student data.** Guide content only.
- **The family take-home** still suggests looking at three photos on a family
  phone. That is a family's own photos, on their own device, under their own
  judgement — a different consent domain from a school projecting children's
  images, and outside this finding.
- **No unrelated curriculum sweep.**

## The lesson

**An activity teaching restraint must not require the thing it warns about.**
The tell is a preparation step: the moment a lesson asks a teacher to *bring*
the material, ask what the material is. Here it was real children. Authored
cards cost the product a few hundred words and remove the sourcing step, the
consent question and the projector exposure at once — and, because they can be
authored, the planted clues can be graded in strength and one of them can be
deliberately clean, which a teacher's own photos could never reliably be.
