# Sprint 22 review — 27 August 2026

**Scope.** The first finding of the second pass, and the first one about the
reporting layer rather than the content. Both halves correct, and they
compounded exactly as described.

Twenty-one sprints have asked whether the missions tell children the truth. This
one asks whether the dashboard tells teachers the truth, and the answer was no.

---

## What was wrong

### The recommendation ran on a metric that only goes up

`mergeEvidence` treats `demonstrated` as sticky. That is deliberate and it is
right for the claim the student and roster labels make — *"has shown this
unaided at least once"*. But it means one good result in any completed mission
hides every later coached one, forever.

Sprint 10 tripled the curriculum specifically so each skill is met in three
different situations. The whole point of that is transfer. And the lifetime
maximum cannot tell **"shown once, then needed coaching twice"** from **"shown
independently every time"** — the two children look identical in every teacher
view there was.

The student and roster copy stayed honest about this. The teacher card did not:
it took the lifetime maximum, called it **Suggested next focus**, and printed
*"X% of this class has shown it so far."* A number that saturates near 100% by
design cannot identify what needs teaching this week.

### The rate divided by the wrong denominator

`CohortSkillStat.demonstratedRate` was documented as *"share of students with
any completed mission touching this skill"* and implemented as `demonstrated /
studentIds.length` — every enrolled student, assigned or not.

So a skill one student of thirty had met, and shown unaided, reported **3%**
rather than 100% of the students who had practised it. `nextTeachingFocus` then
picked the lowest such rate, which means that early in an assignment sequence it
reliably selected **the least-assigned skill**, not the least-understood one.
"Not assigned" was doing arithmetic as though it meant "had a go and failed",
even though the roster is careful to call both of them grey.

A teacher acting on that card reteaches a lesson the class has never had.

## What changed

**Two views, named separately, both honest.**

*Lifetime* is unchanged: `SkillState.result`, sticky, "shown unaided at least
once". *Opportunity* is new: `SkillState.opportunities` is one entry per
completed mission that recorded a result for the skill, oldest first, plus
`demonstratedCount`, `developingCount` and `latest`. **A later coached result is
visible here and does not erase the earlier success.**

**The denominator is fixed.** `demonstratedRate` now divides by
`withOpportunity` — students who have actually met the skill — and the type
exposes that number so every surface can state it. The competency rate had the
same defect (`own.length * studentIds.length`) and got the same correction.

**The recommendation ranks on a signal that can fall.** `independentRate` is
independent choices over total encounters: how often a skill was chosen first go
across every time it has come up. Unlike the lifetime figure it moves in both
directions, which is what makes it something an instructional suggestion can
rest on. The card now reads:

> **Suggested next focus** — Compares a claim against an authoritative source
> Chosen first go **78 of the 109 times** it has come up, across 23 students.

Denominator stated, and it selects a different skill from the one the saturated
metric chose.

**It refuses to make a claim it cannot support.** A skill is only rankable when
at least `MIN_COMPARABLE_GROUP` students *and* at least half the class have had
an opportunity. When nothing qualifies, the card changes its own heading to
**Next unpractised skill** and says so plainly: *"Not enough of this class has
met this skill yet to say anything about how well it is understood."* That is
coverage, and it is no longer dressed as competence.

The class page carries the transfer view under each skill: *"39 of 54 times it
came up, it was chosen first go"*, with the meter label corrected from
`22/23` (of the roster) to `22/23 met it`.

## Tests

Eight, covering exactly the two cases the finding asked for and one it implied:

- A child who demonstrates once and is coached later: lifetime stays
  `demonstrated`, `opportunities` reads `["demonstrated", "developing"]`,
  `latest` is `developing`.
- Shown-every-time versus shown-once-then-coached: **identical under the
  lifetime view**, which the test asserts on purpose, and distinguishable under
  the opportunity view.
- Unequal coverage: one student of thirty gives `demonstratedRate` 1, not 0.03,
  with `notYet` still 29 for the roster.
- That cohort must produce `not-practised`, and must **not** name the skill that
  one student demonstrated.
- Ten students, six independent: produces `reteach` at 0.6 with its denominator.
- Saturation: everybody demonstrates once and is coached once. Lifetime rate is
  exactly 1; independent rate is 0.5; the suggestion must rank below 1.
- Opportunities are counted per encounter, not per student.
- Ordering: opportunities read oldest-first whatever order the rows arrive in.

**335 tests, up from 327.** No new student fields, no telemetry, no per-child
prediction. Everything here is computed from authored attempts and assignments
that already existed.

## What this says about the review process

The content pass ran for eleven sprints and never looked at this. Every guard
written in it asks whether a mission tells a child something true; none asks
whether the roll-up tells a teacher something true, and the roll-up is where a
wrong claim reaches somebody with the power to act on it.

The tell was visible in the code and nobody read it as a defect: the doc comment
on `demonstratedRate` described a different calculation from the one three lines
below it. It had been that way since the function was written. A comment that
disagrees with its implementation is a finding, and I have not been treating
comments as claims to check.

Worth noting what was *not* wrong. The student labels, the roster legend and the
export suppression were all careful and accurate about stickiness. One surface
overreached. That is the usual shape: the honest thing gets built, and then
something downstream quietly promotes it into a claim it cannot carry.

## Deliberately left

- **The rest of the reporting layer has not had this treatment.** The admin
  roll-up, the school report and the CSV export all consume these numbers, and
  they inherit the fix — but nobody has read them for claims of their own.
- **The benchmark roll-up is untouched** and is a separate calculation.
- Every mission has been read once, none twice.
