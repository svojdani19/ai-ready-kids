# Sprint 43 — the roster mutation that did not look like one

**Date:** 2026-08-28
**Scope:** the entitlement bypass sprint 42 reported and left open. Restoration
only.

## The finding

Sprint 42 metered enrolment and excluded archived cohorts from the count. That
exclusion is right: a class kept for retention is not a class being taught, and
charging a new year for children who left in July would mean buying the same desk
twice.

It also opened a door. Archive a full cohort, spend the freed seats on a new one,
restore the old class — and the school is past its licence without a single child
having passed through `createStudent`. **Restoration is a roster mutation that
does not look like one**, which is exactly why the seat check missed it: the
check was written where children are *created*, and this path reactivates
children who already exist.

## The policy, and why this one

Refuse the restore. Keep the class archived, keep every record.

The alternatives are worse for a school:

- **Allow with an overage** is a bill nobody agreed to. On a purchase-order
  subscription there is no mechanism to charge it and no conversation in which
  it is welcome; the school finds out at renewal.
- **Restore partially** would mean software choosing which children come back.
  That is not a decision this product should make.
- **Delete the archived class** to free capacity is the one outcome the
  retention policy exists to prevent.

Refusing leaves the administrator with two ordinary moves — free places by
archiving another cohort, or ask for more — and nothing is lost either way.

## What changed

**`restoreClass` enforces it, not the action.** Same reason as `createStudent`:
the repository is the only door, so a direct call or a future action cannot go
around it. The server action *catches* rather than pre-checks, because asking
first and writing afterwards leaves a window.

**Atomically.** The read and the write share one `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` transaction,
so a restore and an enrolment arriving together cannot both see the same free
seat. On refusal it rolls back and `archived_at` is untouched.

**Exactly on the cap is allowed.** The comparison is `used + roster > licensed`,
never `>=` — a school that paid for 90 seats can have 90 children. A test asserts
the operator, because that is the kind of thing a later edit gets wrong quietly.

**An empty archived class always comes back.** No children, no seats, no reason
to refuse.

**Re-restoring an active class is a no-op, not an overage.** Its students are
already in the active count, and counting them twice would refuse a restore that
changes nothing.

**The refusal states all four numbers and the way out:**

> Room 4 has 21 students, and 69 of 89 licensed places are already in use, so
> restoring it would take this school past its licence. The class stays archived
> and none of its records have changed. Free places by archiving another class,
> or ask Rosa Delgado to request more on the Program and plan page.

The audit records `class.restore_blocked_by_licence` with the class name and the
counts, and **no child's name**. No success audit is written on the refused path.

**One UI change.** `ConfirmAction` rendered its error inline beside the button. A
refusal that has to state four numbers and a route out is a sentence, and a
sentence squeezed beside a button in a table cell is unreadable, so the error is
now a block below it.

## Coverage

Seven tests. The three that assert refusal were confirmed to **fail without the
rule** by stashing the changed source; the rest assert restoration still
*succeeds*, which guards the opposite direction — a cap that over-blocks is as
much a bug as one that under-blocks.

- archive a populated class, fill the freed seats elsewhere, restore refused with
  `archived_at` unchanged on the same timestamp and every record intact;
- the error is both a `RestoreExceedsLicenceError` and a `LicenceExceededError`,
  so a caller that only asks "did the licence say no" still catches it, and it
  carries used, roster and licensed;
- a restore landing exactly on the cap succeeds;
- a zero-student archived class restores whatever the licence says;
- re-restoring an active class is a no-op;
- two schools are measured against their own licences, with one full and the
  other restoring freely;
- the rule is in the repository with `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`, `ROLLBACK` and the `>`
  operator; the action catches, names no child in the audit, and writes the
  success audit only after the refusal path has returned;
- archiving still frees seats for a new cohort, and a refused restore leaves the
  archived records in place for retention.

**501 tests, up from 494.**

## Verified in the browser

At 1280×800 as an administrator, with Room 4 archived (21 students), 69 active
and the school set to 89 seats — one short:

- **Restore was refused** with the message above rendered legibly below the
  button, and the row still tagged **Archived**.
- The database confirmed `archived_at` unchanged at its original timestamp, all
  21 student records intact, the audit entry recording counts only, and **zero**
  `class.restored` entries.
- Raising the licence to 90 — exactly 69 + 21 — the same restore **succeeded**,
  no error, tag gone, 90 active against 90 licensed, and a normal
  `class.restored` audit entry.
- Demo data restored: 120 seats, 90 students, no archived classes.

## Preserved

The PO/invoice model and named account contact, no payment processor and no
billing identifiers, no new student fields, aggregate-only admin reporting, and
every standing constraint. Sprint 42's enrolment metering and the retention
behaviour are unchanged, and both are covered by tests here to prove it.

## The lesson

**A rule enforced where records are created misses every path that reactivates
them.** The seat check was correct and complete for enrolment, and enrolment was
never the only way a child lands on an active roster. Archive and restore are a
pair, and metering one side of a pair is metering nothing: the same reasoning
applies to any future move, merge, transfer or import.

Worth stating plainly, because sprint 42 found this and wrote it up rather than
fixing it: reporting a known bypass is not the same as closing it, and a gap in
`LATEST.md` is still a gap in the product.
