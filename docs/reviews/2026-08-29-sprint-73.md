# Sprint 73 — the curriculum assumed a child already knew what AI was

**Date:** 2026-08-29
**Scope:** First Look, an introductory segment for grades 1 to 5, and the
integration of a second segment into a product that had only ever had one.

---

## What was missing

Mission 1 is called *Sprocket Wants to Know*. Its opening scene hands a child a
tablet, a homework-helper app says hello, and the child is asked whether to type
their full name into it. Every part of that assumes three things:

1. That the thing saying hello is a program which produces what usually comes
   next, not a person and not a friend.
2. That an app on a school tablet is the kind of thing this lesson is about at
   all — that AI is not a robot in a film.
3. That the child, not the app, is the one deciding, and that a person is
   answerable for what happens.

None of the three was ever taught. Twenty-seven missions, an annual benchmark
and a five-module educator orientation, and nowhere did the product tell a child
what AI is. A seven year old who thinks Sprocket is alive can still pick the
safe answer in that mission, and the roster will record `privacy.identity`
demonstrated. What they have learned is that this particular cartoon gear is
untrustworthy.

The second gap is grade range. The programme claimed grades 2 to 4 everywhere,
and class creation refused any other grade. A grade 1 teacher and a grade 5
teacher could not create a class at all.

## What was built

**Six First Look sessions in two grade tiers**, `src/content/foundations/`.

| Track | Grades | Sessions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early | 1–2 | The Guessing Machine · You Have Met It Already · Who Does the Thinking? |
| Upper | 3–5 | Where the Guesses Come From · Spot the AI · Who Is in Charge Here |

Both tiers teach the same three ideas. They are not the same script at two
lengths: a ten year old handed the grade 1 version hears it as babyish and stops
listening, which is the failure this split exists to avoid.

- **Early** starts inside the children's own heads. The class completes
  "peanut butter and ___", notices *why* they could, and only then meets a
  program doing the same thing from millions of sentences. It closes on the
  program never having met them.
- **Upper** starts with a folk theory and replaces it with a mechanism. Room 20
  asks the school's writing helper about the Brightwood swimming team. Brightwood
  has no pool. The answer arrives in two seconds with a coach, a practice night
  and a trophy, and a student says the line the session is built around: *it did
  not look wrong, it looked like every other answer.*

Cast: the early track stays in Room 12 with Ms. Okafor, the room the core
missions are set in. The upper track moves one room along to Room 20 with
Mr. Alvarez, so a fifth grader is not asked to identify with the younger room.

**Grades widened to 1–5.** Class creation offers grades 1 to 5, the action
accepts them, and `trackForGrade` routes a class to the tier written for it.
The twenty-seven core missions still say `2-4` on every library card, preview
and printable guide, because that is the band they are written and
reading-levelled for and the product should not imply otherwise.

## The decision that shaped the integration

**First Look records no skill evidence, anywhere.**

The alternative was tempting and wrong. A First Look session obviously *touches*
privacy, verification and ownership, and it would have been easy to have it
record against the nine skills. But a six year old answering "no, it has never
met Nia's cat" on a projector is a comprehension check. A child declining to
type their street address into an app that has just offered them Turbo Mode is a
safety decision under pressure. Putting both in a column headed "demonstrated
this skill" makes the column mean two different things, and the teacher reading
the roster cannot tell which one they are looking at.

So the evidence rule **inverts** for this segment. `validateMission` normally
flags a strong choice that records nothing; for `segment: "foundation"` it flags
any choice that records anything. That lives in the validator rather than in a
content review, so a session added next year is checked by machinery rather than
by memory.

The consequences were followed through rather than left implicit:

- `MISSIONS` still means the assessed spine — twenty-seven, interleaved, nine
  skills, three encounters each. Every interleaving and spacing test still runs
  against exactly that set. `ALL_SESSIONS` is the new name for "anything a child
  can open", and the slug and id maps are built from it.
- `offeredBy`, the denominator a teacher reads as "chances to show this skill",
  is unchanged. A First Look session that named a skill without recording it
  would have inflated it and made classes look as though they had missed
  opportunities they were never given.
- The school report gained a `segment` column rather than a footnote, in the
  screen table and in the CSV, because a "· First Look" suffix on a title
  survives a screen and does not survive a spreadsheet.
- "Every mission in use" on the administrator's checklist became "every **core**
  mission in use". No school runs both grade tracks, so the old check could
  never have been completed again.

## Classroom review

Run against the running app at 1280×800 and 768×1024, as a grade 2 student
(Room 4, early track), a grade 3 student (Room 12, upper track), the teacher and
the administrator.

### Findings, all fixed in this sprint

1. **The player headed a First Look session "Mission 2".** The child taps a tile
   that says FIRST LOOK 2 and lands on a page that says Mission 2. At this age
   that reads as having opened the wrong thing. The eyebrow, and Classroom
   Mode's, now follow the segment.

2. **The printable discussion guide said "Primary skill recorded:" on a session
   that records nothing.** This was the worst of the findings: it is the sheet a
   teacher prints, keeps and plans from, so it is where a false claim would have
   survived longest. It now reads "Records nothing. Leads into: …".

3. **Session numbering ran 1 to 6 across both tracks.** A grade 5 class was
   offered sessions 4, 5 and 6 and the printable footer said "Session 6 of 3".
   `order` is now per track, so each tier is 1 to 3 and a teacher is not left
   hunting for the first three. `tests/foundations.test.ts` asserts it.

4. **"Ms. Okafor points at four things instead", followed by three options.** A
   six year old counts. Narration corrected.

5. **The family take-home for a First Look session was filed under a competency
   heading**, which put "Learning Ownership" on a page for a session that
   assesses nothing. Now headed "First Look".

6. **The two track columns on the public curriculum page collided at 768.** Each
   card sat at about 350px and session titles wrapped to three lines. Moved from
   `md:` to `lg:`.

7. **A 25-word sentence in the grades 3–5 tier**, one over the cap. Split.

8. **The mission preview labelled a First Look session with a primary skill.**
   Now "Leads into", with an explicit note saying the session appears on no
   roster and no report.

### Checked and found sound

- Retry loops, endings, badge award and the save-state messaging on all six
  sessions; a rethink choice loops back and records nothing, as everywhere else.
- Read-aloud is present on every First Look scene.
- Student map: First Look sits above the three competency lanes with its own
  count, and the header total counts both segments (`8 of 12` for a grade 2
  child, not `8 of 9`).
- Badge wall shows only the track the class's grade maps to, plus any session
  from the other track the child has actually finished — no permanently locked
  row a child has no way to unlock.
- Classroom Mode drives a First Look session with the same keys, the same branch
  reveal and the same hideable notes.
- No horizontal overflow at 768×1024 on the student map or the curriculum page;
  the only sub-44px targets are the pre-existing skip link and an inline text
  link.

## Deliberately left

- **The core missions are still written for grades 2 to 4.** A grade 5 class can
  be created, gets the upper First Look track, and can be assigned core missions
  — but those are reading-levelled for 2–4 and every teacher-facing surface says
  so rather than pretending otherwise. Upper-grade variants of the twenty-seven
  are a content sprint, not a wiring change, and shipping a grade band claim
  ahead of the content would be the dishonest half of this.
- **No grade 1 or grade 5 demo class.** The demo school stays at four classes so
  the seeded figures in the annual report stay comparable across sprints. Every
  demo class does now open with the First Look track for its grade.
- **The benchmark is untouched.** It measures the nine skills, First Look does
  not teach them, and adding introductory items would change what the fall and
  spring windows are comparing.
- **Two pre-existing lint warnings** on `(site)/privacy/page.tsx` for unused
  imports. Not this sprint's, not fixed in this sprint's diff.

## Verification

`npm run verify` — typecheck, lint, 790 tests across 24 files, production build.
`tests/foundations.test.ts` is new: 30 tests covering structural validity, the
records-nothing rule at three levels, the per-band reading caps, per-track
numbering, the self-limiting extensions and the blanket-distrust check.
