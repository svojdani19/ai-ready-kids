# Sprint 21 review — 27 August 2026

**Scope.** The last unread original mission. It had findings, which makes it
**twenty-six of twenty-seven**. Every mission in the curriculum has now been
read by a person, and only The Art Show Label came back clean.

Both findings here are about the mission's central artefact — the four doors,
which the wall poster, three other missions and a shared art asset all point at.
Getting it wrong got it wrong in several places at once.

---

## Finding 1 — the first card scored arithmetic and called it strategy

The card said only *seven plus eight*. The full-credit feedback then asserted a
learner state the story never established: *"You already know this one, or you
nearly do."* Reaching for a tool was a **retry**, because *"you can do seven
plus eight."* Asking a person earned only `developing`.

For a lot of second graders that is simply false, and for a child with
dyscalculia, a working-memory difference, maths anxiety, interrupted schooling
or an agreed calculator arrangement it is false and unkind at once. A child
could answer honestly for their own situation and be recorded below mastery for
their numeracy rather than for their strategy selection — which is not the skill
the scene claims to assess.

The mission also contradicted itself. Its own final reflection says *"the same
problem can need a different door next week — seven plus eight is a think-it-out
for you now and was an ask-a-person in year one."* Scene two scored it as fixed.

### What changed

**The state is now in the story**, so the scoring has something real to assess:

> *"You worked this one out yesterday, on paper, and got there. Today it is
> sitting right on the edge of your head, nearly back."*

Thinking it out is right *for where you are today* — "reaching past a fact that
is on its way would stop it arriving". Asking an AI tool is a retry about **this
card, today**, and says so: *"That is about this card today, not about what you
can do."*

And there is a second full-credit answer, which is the fix that matters:

> **"Ask for a hint, or use whatever I have agreed with Ms. Okafor"** — *"A hint
> gets you the last step without handing you the answer. And if you and Ms.
> Okafor have agreed you use something for number facts, that is your
> arrangement, not a shortcut, and today does not change it."*

The guide says it plainly to teachers: an agreed accommodation is not the
shortcut this mission is about, and a particular child's speed or support is
never discussed in front of the room.

## Finding 2 — the taxonomy overlapped, and the activity logged children

**The doors were not mutually exclusive.** THINK IT OUT / LOOK IT UP / ASK A
PERSON / **USE A TOOL** — but looking something up uses a tool, a calculator is
a tool, a book is a tool, and so is software that reads a page aloud. The
mission then silently narrowed "tool" to mean an AI system in later scenes.
Overlapping labels make this a vocabulary test rather than a routine.

Door four is now **ASK AN AI TOOL**, and Ms. Okafor says the rest out loud
before anybody chooses:

> *"Door four means the AI tools this school lets us use. Not any old one."*
> *"And a tool can help you at any of these doors. A book is a tool. So is a
> calculator, and so is the thing that reads to you. The doors are about where
> the answer comes from, not about what you are holding."*

**The doors also stack.** Scene three treated look-it-up and ask-a-tool as
competitors, while the guide quietly conceded that a tool naming a source should
be followed. That concession is now in the mission: *"If it says where it got
it, go and read that yourself. Then you have used two doors in a row, which is
allowed and often the quickest way there."* The wrap-up carries it.

**The extension was behavioural tracking.** *"Keep a door tally for a week. Each
time a student gets stuck they mark which door they used."* That is a running
record of which children need help, how often, and what they reach for. This
product keeps no behavioural telemetry — `tests/access-control.test.ts` asserts
that at the schema level — and a paper version in a classroom is not better for
being on paper. It also makes help-seeking a thing that gets watched, which is
the opposite of what the mission wants.

It is now six teacher-written situations, the same six for everybody, sorted by
show of hands or by walking to a corner, with the time spent arguing about the
ones that split. Nothing is written down and nobody's own week comes into it.

## Guards added

Eight tests. The one worth keeping is a **sweep over every mission's extension**:
no classroom activity may accumulate per-child help-seeking over time. Noticing
something in the moment is what `lookFor` is for and stays fine; keeping a
register does not.

That guard turned up nothing else today, which is the first sweep here to come
back empty. Worth recording, because a sweep that finds nothing is still doing
its job — it now applies to every extension written from here.

**327 tests, up from 321.**

## What this says about the review process

Twenty-seven missions, twenty-six with findings. That is the honest number and
it is worth writing down plainly rather than framing as progress.

The two clean-ish conclusions from the whole pass:

**The defects were not randomly distributed.** Five were inclusion failures —
scoring a child's family, a classmate's talent, a hand's writing, a familiar
adult's character, and now a second grader's arithmetic. Every one was written
warmly, read plausibly as a sentence, and failed the moment somebody asked which
child it fails. That question is now four separate items on the checklist, and
it should have been the first one.

**Guides described intent; scenes did the teaching.** More than once the
discussion guide stated the correct principle while the scenes taught the
opposite, and the evidence awards followed the scenes. Reviewing by reading
setups would have passed those missions. It has to be the scenes, and the paths
between them.

## Deliberately left

- **Every mission has now been read once.** None has been read twice, and the
  findings did not slow down as the pass went on.
- **Nothing checks what a wrong answer costs a child** — flagged in sprint 20
  and still true. The evidence model is well tested for integrity and not at all
  for whether the honest answer is reachable by everybody.
- **Shared scenes remain untraced** in twenty-six of twenty-seven missions.
- Teacher-facing copy still promises timelines it cannot support.
