# Sprint 42 — the subscription was a label the customer could edit

**Date:** 2026-08-28
**Scope:** one bounded entitlement sprint. Not a payment build, not a district
platform.

## The finding

Two defects that only look serious together.

**The customer could raise its own paid entitlement.** The administrator form
said *Request a quote* and its own note said it *"records an intent"*. But
`requestPlanChangeAction` ran `UPDATE schools SET plan = ?, licensed_students =
?` immediately. Typing 5000 into a field labelled as a request made the school a
5000-seat district customer as far as every screen in the product was concerned.

**Nothing read the number back.** `addStudentAction` never consulted
`licensed_students`, so teachers could enrol without limit across every class.

Either alone is a bug. Together they mean the seat count is simultaneously
self-editable and unenforced — which is to say it is decoration. For a school
buyer, the pricing, the invoice seat count and the "licensed students" figure are
all unreliable, and a vendor cannot run a purchase-order subscription on it
without arguing after the fact about what was sold.

## The commercial rationale

This product is bought by schools on a purchase order: a quote, a PO, an invoice,
a named account contact. There is no card and there should not be one. That makes
the *entitlement record* the entire commercial mechanism — it is the only thing
standing between "what we agreed" and "what you are using". So it has to be true
in both directions:

- **The vendor owns the number.** What a school has bought is not something the
  school types in. A customer-editable entitlement cannot appear on an invoice,
  and a renewal conversation that starts by disputing the seat count is a
  conversation that costs more than the seat.
- **The number has to bite.** An entitlement nothing enforces is a suggestion.
  Seat counts that drift silently upward are how a vendor discovers at renewal
  that it has been giving away a third of its product, and how a school
  discovers it is being asked for money it never budgeted.

Neither half is about restricting schools. It is about both sides being able to
point at the same figure a year later.

## What changed

**The quote request records a request and nothing else.** The `UPDATE` is gone.
The action writes one audit entry naming what was asked for *and what the
entitlement still is*, and the success message says so in plain words: *"Your
plan is still school with 91 licensed students, and it stays that way until a new
agreement is in place. Nothing has been charged and nothing has changed."*

**The entitlement is presented read-only, with the request visibly separate.**
The Subscription panel now shows current plan and seats in their own read-only
block — *"Set by your agreement and changed by your account contact, not from
this page. The fields below ask for something different; they do not grant it."*
The fields are relabelled *Plan you would like* and *Student places you would
like*. The stat at the top of the page is now **used of licensed** with the
remainder, rather than a bare number.

**The cap is enforced in the repository, not the action.** `createStudent` does
the check itself, so it is the only door. Sprint 26 learned this about
authorization — a rule enforced in the page that renders the button is not a
rule — and a seat limit a future action could route around is one a buyer cannot
rely on. The server action *catches* rather than pre-checks, because an action
that asked first and inserted afterwards would leave a window between the two.

**Atomically.** The count and the insert run inside one `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` write
transaction, so two enrolments arriving together cannot both read the same count
and both write. On refusal it rolls back and nothing is written at all.

**Seats in use = children on the roster of an active class.** Archived cohorts
are excluded deliberately. A school keeps last year's classes for its retention
period; a cohort held for records is not a child being taught. Charging a new
year for children who left in July would mean buying the same desk twice and
would push administrators toward deleting records early to free capacity — the
exact opposite of what the retention policy is for.

**The last licensed seat succeeds.** A cap that blocks *at* the number sold sells
one fewer than it says. Number 91 of 91 goes through; 92 does not.

**The refusal is useful and names no child:**

> 91 of 91 licensed student places are in use, so this school cannot enrol
> anybody else yet. Nothing was added. Ask Rosa Delgado to request more places on
> the Program and plan page.

Seats used, seats licensed, and the programme contact path. The school-wide audit
records `roster.blocked_by_licence` with the class and the numbers and **no
child's name** — a licence event is a fact about the school.

**Adding to an archived class is refused.** Archived rosters do not consume
seats, so if they also accepted children, archiving a full class would be a way
to keep enrolling and restore them afterwards.

## Acceptance coverage

Eight tests in `tests/admin-journey.test.ts`, all confirmed to **fail without the
enforcement** by stashing the changed source and re-running:

- a quote request leaves plan and seats unchanged while recording the request,
  asserted both against the action's source and by exercising what it now does;
- students in two active classes combine toward one school cap;
- the final licensed seat succeeds, the next fails, **no row is created and no
  success audit is written**;
- archived cohorts do not consume a new year's seats, and their records survive;
- enrolling into an archived cohort is refused;
- two schools count separately and each cap binds only its own;
- the check is in the repository with `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` and `ROLLBACK`, the
  action catches rather than pre-checks, and the refusal audit contains no
  display name;
- the student record still holds exactly `id`, `class_id`, `display_name`,
  `avatar_key`, `created_at`.

**494 tests, up from 486.** One test-harness change: `createTestDb` now licenses
the fixture generously, because almost no test in the suite is about entitlement
and many enrol far more children than the demo school buys. Tests that mean to
exercise the cap call the new `setLicensedSeats` and say the number out loud.

## Verified in the browser

At 1280×800, signed in as each role on the running dev server, with the school
temporarily set to 91 seats against a 90-child roster:

- The program page shows **Plan: Whole school**, **Student places: 90 of 91, 1
  left**, and the read-only entitlement block above the request fields.
- Submitting a request for **district / 5000** returned the unchanged-entitlement
  message, and the database row still read `plan: school, licensed_students: 91`.
  The audit entry records the request and the unchanged figure.
- As a teacher, the 91st child was added normally. The 92nd was refused with the
  message above; the database confirmed **no row** for it, and the audit showed
  `roster.blocked_by_licence` naming the class and the numbers only.
- Demo data restored afterwards: 90 students, 120 seats.

## Deliberately not built

- **No card fields, no payment processor, no billing identifiers.** Purchase
  orders, quotes, invoices and the named account contact are the mechanism, and
  they stay that way.
- **Lapsed-subscription read-only enforcement**, left for a separate review as
  instructed. The renewal note on the program page still says nothing switches
  off automatically, which remains true.
- **Student fields, aggregate-only admin reporting, the privacy constraints and
  the authored AI-readiness niche** are all untouched.

## Known gap, for the next review

**Restoring an archived class can cross the cap.** Archiving frees seats; if a
school archives a full cohort, enrols a new one, and then restores the old class,
the active roster can exceed the licence. `restoreClassAction` does not check.
This needs a product decision rather than a guess — refuse the restore, allow it
and report an overage, or restore read-only — so it is reported rather than
invented. The enrolment path itself cannot be used to get there, because adding
to an archived class is refused.

## The lesson

**A number that a customer can edit and that nothing reads is not a control, it
is a caption.** Both halves were needed and each looked defensible on its own:
the write was "just recording what they asked for", and the missing check was
"we don't enforce limits in an MVP". The product read as a subscription business
in every screen and was not one in any line of code. Where a field is the
commercial mechanism, the test is whether a vendor and a school could both point
at it a year later and agree what it means.
