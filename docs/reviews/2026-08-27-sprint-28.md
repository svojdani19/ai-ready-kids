# Sprint 28 review — 27 August 2026

**Scope.** Route and action audit, continued. Both findings correct. The first
breaks the promise the whole product rests on.

---

## Finding 1 — the authored graph was enforced by the browser

The central claim of this product is that every path is authored and
safety-reviewed. The server checked that a submitted scene and choice existed
**somewhere in the mission**, and `recordDecision` appended whatever it was
handed. Nothing compared the scene against the one the stored path actually
leads to.

`finishMission` was worse: `completeAttempt` took the caller's word that a
mission was finished, without replaying the path to see whether it reached an
ending.

So a direct caller could post the strongest option from every decision scene in
any order — skipping every story beat, skipping every authored correction — come
out with a full set of `demonstrated` evidence, mark the mission complete, and
collect the badge. Teacher evidence, the roster and the annual report would then
describe a mission nobody played.

The check-in had the same terminal defect. `completeBenchmark` marked a form
finished with **zero responses**, which counted that child into the cohort and
scored every unanswered item as incorrect. And a completed form could still be
written to, so an answer already counted into a school figure could be revised
afterwards.

### The fix

Two pure functions in `missionPath.ts`:

- `expectedDecisionSceneId(mission, path)` — the one decision this attempt may
  record next, derived by replaying the stored path and walking forward through
  story scenes. Retries keep you on the same scene, which is what makes the
  authored corrections work.
- `hasReachedEnding(mission, path)` — whether replaying actually arrives at an
  ending.

**The invariants live in `src/lib/repo/progress.ts`, not in the action.** The
action is one caller; the repository is the boundary. `recordDecision` refuses a
scene that is not the expected one and refuses any write to a completed attempt.
`completeAttempt` refuses unless the path reaches an ending.
`saveBenchmarkResponse` refuses a finished form. `completeBenchmark` requires
**one valid saved answer for every authored item** before it will mark anything
complete.

### What this broke, and why that was the point

Six existing tests failed immediately, all of them fabricating attempts a child
could never have. They posted straight at a scene, or completed a mission from
its opening. Each was rewritten to walk the mission — there is a `playTo` and a
`playToEnd` helper now — rather than loosened.

One deserves naming. `keeps demonstrated sticky once earned unaided elsewhere`
had been posting the *same* choice at the *same* scene twice, which is not what
"elsewhere" means and is impossible in play. It now searches for a mission that
records one skill at two different scenes, earns it cleanly at the first, gets
coached at the second, and asserts the record does not fall. That is the claim
the test was named for, tested for the first time.

Another was replaced rather than repaired: `keeps demonstrated evidence sticky
against a later weaker answer` posted to a finished attempt. Stickiness is
asserted properly elsewhere now, so it became the stronger claim — **a finished
attempt cannot be written to at all**, and neither path nor evidence moves when
somebody tries.

## Finding 2 — authorised on the class, acted on the student

`removeStudentAction` called `requireOwnClass(classId)` and then
`deleteStudent(studentId)`. The two ids were never checked against each other.

A teacher could pass their own legitimate class id alongside any student id they
knew and permanently delete that child — from a colleague's class, or another
school — with every cascaded attempt and check-in going with them, while the
audit entry named the teacher's own class.

`deleteStudent` is now `deleteStudentFromClass(db, id, classId)`, scoped by both
ids in the SQL and **returning whether a row actually went**. The action refuses
a mismatch before the audit is written, so a deletion that did not happen cannot
leave a success entry behind claiming it did.

This is sprint 27's shape one level deeper: the thing checked and the thing acted
on were different objects.

## Tests

Twelve new. The progression ones assert the attack rather than the absence of it:

- A decision at a scene the path has not reached is refused, and the path stays
  empty.
- A decision at a scene the path has already left is refused.
- **Posting every strong choice in reverse order** accepts at most one — the
  genuine first decision — and the attempt then refuses to complete.
- Finishing from the opening scene is refused and no badge is issued.
- A mission actually played to an ending completes.
- An empty check-in cannot be completed; a partly answered one cannot either;
  a fully answered one completes and then **locks against revision**.

And the delete ones: same-class succeeds, a colleague's student is refused with
nothing changed, an unknown id reports failure, and the action's own body must
check `removed` before reaching `recordAudit`.

**393 tests, up from 381.**

## What this says about the review process

Twenty-seven sprints of review took the authored graph as given. It is the
premise of every content sprint — the reason there is no chatbot, the reason
every branch is reviewed, the reason "deterministic, safety-reviewed branches"
is the first line of the product description. Nothing ever checked that the
server enforced it. The `MissionPlayer` component did, faithfully, and a
component is a suggestion.

The pattern from sprint 27 held exactly: **existence is not entitlement**, and
here also *existence is not sequence*. Every check asked whether a thing was
real. None asked whether it was next.

There is a second, quieter lesson in what the fix broke. Six tests were asserting
against states no user could produce, and they had been green for twenty-eight
sprints. A test that constructs an impossible state proves nothing about the
system and actively hides the missing rule — the fabrication *is* the bug,
written down and passing. When an invariant lands and tests go red, the first
question is whether they were describing something real.

## Deliberately left

- **`simulateAttempt` in the seed still writes paths directly**, bypassing
  `recordDecision`. It generates demo data and is not reachable by a user, but
  it means the seeded paths are not themselves proof of anything.
- **No route audit is complete.** Three sprints have each found what they were
  pointed at. Nobody has enumerated every route and action and asked, for each,
  what it checks and what it acts on.
