# Sprint 15 review — 27 August 2026

**Scope.** Two findings from a human read of the next two Sprint 09 missions.
Both correct. Ten missions read by hand now, ten with findings.

Both needed the decision tree changed. One needed the mission restructured.

---

## Finding 1 — "The Perfect Drawing" taught peer profiling as evidence

The mission's whole verification arc ran through what the child knew about Nia.
*"This does not match anything else she has made"* earned `demonstrated`.
*"She probably practised"* was a forced retry. *"Do you have the first tries?"*
earned full credit on the claim that **"real drawings leave a trail"**, and the
wrap-up repeated it as a rule. The guide said outright that the mission
"deliberately gives the strongest clue as knowledge of Nia."

None of that is provenance. A sudden jump in quality and an absence of visible
drafts are not evidence about anything. Children get better fast; they practise
privately, watch a video, switch to a medium that suits them, receive legitimate
help, or use a tool the school gave them. Work arrives without rough copies
because it was done on a screen, or done once, or the rough copies went in the
bin.

And the scene that asked Nia to demonstrate her technique assumed an honest
artist would "show you, happily, for ten minutes." A child who is shy, who finds
talking hard, who is still learning English, or whose hands do not do what they
are told may not be able to perform on request when the work is entirely their
own. That option punished exactly the children least able to answer it.

For seven- to ten-year-olds, all of this converts *verify the work* into
**judge whether your classmate seems capable of it** — and awards mastery for
doing so.

### What changed

**The universal process question is now the starting norm, in scene one.** The
entry form carries HOW DID YOU MAKE THIS? before Nia's dragon appears, and Ms.
Okafor says every answer is a fine answer: pencil, paint, an app, my uncle
helped with the wings. It is not a remedy invented after a class scrutinised one
child.

The decision scene inverted. Somebody else says "there is no way she drew that",
and that is now the **retry** — "a guess about a person", with a coach note
saying this is the failure the mission exists to prevent and is more likely than
the one about the dragon. *"It is surprising. That is all it is"* is `partial`:
being surprised is allowed, people do get suddenly better, and surprise is
something to be curious about rather than something that tells you anything. The
only full-credit answer is that the form asks everybody, **so nobody has to be
guessed about**.

The draft-hunting choice is gone. The kind question is now the neutral one every
entrant gets — *"That is amazing. What did you make it with?"* — and going to
Ms. Okafor to check one person's entry is `partial`: she checks all of them the
same way, and singling one out "is the thing that would follow Nia round for a
year."

Nia still answers honestly, still is never punished, and still is never actually
caught: she answers the question everybody answers. Disclosed tool use is still
fine, which was always the best thing in this mission.

The teacher setup now carries the whole inclusion argument, and a misconception
answers the sentence a class will say: *"But she has never drawn like that
before."* The extension says to ask every child, "not the ones whose work
surprised you — asking only the surprising ones is the whole problem in
miniature." The family summary is corrected: the form was still blank, so Nia
had not entered anything as her own.

## Finding 2 — "The Sleepover Screen" finished socially and stopped there

Two gaps, and the second is the one that matters.

**Inventing a school earned full credit** while the child's real name was
already in the app. That teaches *substitute a detail and keep playing* as the
privacy remedy. Closing the app is now the full-credit move — "you do not have
to decide about this question, and then the next one, and the one after that" —
and the made-up school is `partial`: it keeps one thing out, the app is still
running, the real name is still in there, and "a made-up answer is something to
agree with your own grown-up first, not a trick to work out on your own." Sam
still types Banana Academy, because the joke was the best line in the mission.

**Telling Bea earned mastery and changed nothing.** She said she was glad you
mentioned it, and the story moved on with the name still in the app. The
full-credit sentence now carries the whole routine: *"It asked for our names and
schools. My name is in it. Can you take it out?"* — what went in, and what you
want done about it. The old question, *"Is that okay?"*, is `partial`: a good
question one step short, because "she has no way to know your name is already in
there unless you tell her."

A new scene makes the routine actually happen. Bea closes the app, puts the
tablet on the shelf, deletes the character with the child's name on it, **says
she cannot tell whether the app kept anything**, and messages the child's own
grown-up to let them decide. That last part is the piece a child cannot supply
and must be shown.

**"I can say no and go home"** was not an action a seven-year-old can initiate
at a sleepover. It is now *"I can put it down and ask to ring my grown-up"*,
with the body giving the whole bounded routine: put the tablet down, go and be
somewhere else in the house, ask to phone, and you never have to explain first.
The family sheet asks parents to agree that half out loud.

## Guards added

Eight tests, plus three phrases banned across the whole curriculum:
`real drawings leave a trail`, `real work leaves a trail`,
`she will show you, happily`.

265 tests, up from 257.

## What this says about the review process

Ten read, ten with findings.

The first finding is the second inclusion defect in two sprints, after the
familiar-adult exoneration in sprint 14. Both were written warmly, both awarded
mastery for the wrong reason, and both would have taught a class something about
people rather than about evidence. That is now a pattern with two instances, and
it is worth naming as a category rather than treating each as a one-off: **when
a mission's check runs through a person, look at who it disadvantages.**

The checklist gains an item for it.

## Deliberately left

- **Ten Sprint 09 missions unread.** Down from twelve, still the dominant risk.
- **The eight legacy forced-award scenes**, still untraced.
- No general guard against factual error exists or can.
