# Sprint 09 review — 26 August 2026

**Scope.** Triple the curriculum, from nine missions to twenty-seven. Also a
positive reframe of the landing page's claims section.

**Method.** Ran against the checklist in `../classroom-review.md`. Every new
mission is validated by the existing structural suite — reachability,
termination, feedback coverage, retry-on-unsafe, evidence validity, reading
level, banned vocabulary, no benchmark overlap — rather than by inspection.
Browser-checked the curriculum page, the badge wall and a new mission in the
player.

## What changed

### Eighteen new missions, three per skill

The curriculum was one mission per skill. It is now three, and `order` runs in
skill triplets rather than sweeping across a competency once, so a teacher
assigning in sequence gets spaced practice without planning for it.

| Skill | Missions |
| --- | --- |
| Withholds identifying information | Sprocket Wants to Know · The Quiz That Kept Asking · The Study Group |
| Evaluates photo and location requests | The Filter That Wanted More · The Class Photo · What the Camera Sees |
| Stops and involves a trusted adult | The Question at Bedtime · The Sleepover Screen · It Happened to Theo |
| Separates confidence from accuracy | The Very Sure Answer · The Book That Was Not There · The Helper and the Teacher |
| Identifies possibly synthetic media | The Penguin on the Playground · The Video of Mr. Ruiz · The Perfect Drawing |
| Checks against an authoritative source | Two Answers, One Truth · The Science Fair Fact · The Weather Argument |
| Attempts the task before asking | The Homework That Did Itself · The Story That Was Not Mine · The Practice That Got Skipped |
| Selects an appropriate strategy | Four Doors · The Group Project · The Question With No Answer |
| Reports assistance accurately | The Spelling Test Surprise · The Art Show Label · The Reading Log |

Each new mission works a situation none of the others use: security questions
dressed as a quiz, a routine given away in a study group, consent inside a
class photo, a live camera in a bedroom, a guest in somebody else's house, a
friend's disclosure, an invented book, a tool contradicting a teacher, a fake
video of a familiar adult, a generated picture entered as hand-drawn, a myth
that three sources copied from one, a forecast contradicting a window, a
better story that took the interesting part, a nightly shortcut nobody would
catch, a group plan that empties the group, a question no source can settle, a
label on an art show card, and a log only the child can verify.

### Every new decision has real alternatives

This is the direct answer to the Sprint 07 finding, applied to new work rather
than retrofitted. **Every decision scene in the eighteen new missions has at
least two non-looping exits**, so the evidence a child earns reflects a choice
they made rather than the only door out of the room. The eight legacy
forced-award scenes remain scheduled in `LATEST.md`; nothing new joins them.

### Badges became families

Twenty-seven badges would have meant eighteen falling back to one default
glyph. The glyph is now keyed to the skill a mission builds, so the three
badges for a skill are visibly a set. A child collecting them can see what they
are collecting, which twenty-seven unrelated drawings would not have shown.

### Landing page claims, reframed

*"What a school can honestly claim"* opened with a negation and sat above three
cards that each began with "No". Same facts, now stated as what is true: *What
your school can show*, *A class code is all they need*, *Evidence, not
surveillance*, *It never asks for a permission*. The honesty is unchanged; it
no longer arrives apologetically.

## Findings during the review

- **One sentence over the reading-level cap.** Caught by the suite, not by
  reading: a 34-word sentence in *It Happened to Theo*. Split into three. The
  test earned its keep on the first run against new content.
- **Nine hardcoded assumptions.** Tests asserting `toHaveLength(9)`, seed
  assignment counts, and marketing copy saying "nine missions" in seven places.
  Test assertions now derive from `MISSIONS.length` and from the seeded data,
  so the next content sprint will not have to repeat this.
- **Seed rebalanced.** Classes now carry 18, 15, 11 and 9 assignments rather
  than all nine, which reads as a school partway through a year rather than one
  that has finished. 869 seeded attempts, up from 472.

---

## Checked and found acceptable

- **No repetition.** Each mission has its own situation, its own misconception
  pair, and its own family rule. The recurring cast is deliberate continuity;
  the scenarios are not.
- **No benchmark overlap.** No new mission reuses a check-in situation, so both
  windows still measure transfer rather than recall.
- **Reading level.** Every student-facing sentence, including feedback bodies,
  passes the 32-word cap. Banned vocabulary suite-wide.
- **Standing constraints.** No generative model, no free text from children, no
  new student fields, no telemetry. Nothing in eighteen missions touches these.
- **Student map still readable.** It shows assigned missions only, so a class
  with nine assigned sees nine cards regardless of the catalogue size.

---

## Deliberately left

- **Nine glyphs for twenty-seven badges.** A family look was chosen over
  bespoke drawings. If badges ever need to be individually recognisable —
  printed on stickers, say — this needs revisiting.
- **No unit or term grouping in the data model.** `order` carries the sequence
  and the teacher assigns what they want. If schools start asking for
  half-term bundles, that is a schema change and should be one.
- **The curriculum page is long.** Nine missions per competency in a three-
  column grid. Acceptable for an evaluator reading once; worth revisiting if it
  becomes a working reference for teachers.
