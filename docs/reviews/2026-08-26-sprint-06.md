# Sprint 06 review — 26 August 2026

**Scope.** Mission 5, *The Penguin on the Playground*, and the shared
synthetic-media competency language. The review asked whether a child could
finish the mission believing that careful eyes are a reliable fake detector,
even though the teacher guide says provenance is the durable skill.

**Method.** Compared the mission's learning goals, evidence awards, teacher
guide, family activity, badge and both benchmark transfer items. Drove the
changed decision in the independent player at 768×1024 and Classroom Mode at
1280×800. Checked reading load, viewport fit, action visibility and browser
errors, then ran the complete automated verification suite.

---

## Finding and what changed

### Pixel-hunting earned the same evidence as checking the real world — fixed

The decision asks for the *strongest* clue in a suspicious penguin photo, but
all three answers previously earned full demonstrated evidence:

- the child was outside and knows it did not snow;
- the penguin has no shadow;
- the slide appears on the wrong side.

That made a missing shadow as instructionally strong as first-hand knowledge.
It also conflicted with the mission's own teacher guidance: generated media
will improve, so provenance must outlast visual artefact detection.

The evidence ladder is now explicit:

- **First-hand context demonstrates the skill.** The child was there, is a
  witness, and can ask another person who was outside.
- **A visual detail is developing evidence.** A missing shadow or changed
  layout is a reason to pause, not proof. Real photos can be cropped, flipped or
  oddly lit; generated images can have convincing details.
- **The next move is always a trail.** Ask who made the image, who saw the event
  or where the claim can be checked.

The badge is now **Trail Checker**, replacing **Sharp Eyes**. The shared skill
label likewise measures whether a child checked who made or saw the media,
rather than whether they believed they could spot it. The teacher extension no
longer asks children to guess real versus generated images from appearance; it
uses familiar classroom images to practise three source questions.

The voice-copy feedback also dropped the brittle claim about an exact number of
recorded seconds. The durable point is that a short recording can be enough and
the real person is the check.

### Independent review found a reporting leak — fixed for this mission

Claude's independent review found that the later voice scene had only one
non-retry answer and that answer awarded demonstrated evidence. Every child who
finished the mission therefore ended with demonstrated evidence, even after
choosing only weaker reasoning when a real alternative existed.

The forced answer now teaches and confirms the safe action without recording
evidence. Mission 5 evidence comes from the two earlier decisions where the
child has a genuine choice. This makes the teacher-facing result match the
child's reasoning: source or first-hand checks can demonstrate the skill, while
an unhelpful first question plus a visual clue remains developing.

The same review caught two smaller content slips. Student-facing copy now avoids
the editing terms *mismatch*, *flipped* and *cropped* (including one older
Mission 2 headline), and the family summary no longer claims that the story
proved the untraceable penguin photo was made by a computer.

## Content safeguard added

A regression test now asserts that first-hand context earns demonstrated
evidence while the two artefact-only choices remain partial/developing and
direct the child to pause or check. It also confirms that the forced voice
answer records no evidence and that all student copy avoids the three editing
terms. This protects the instructional hierarchy, not just the presence of
feedback text. The structural validator now permits a strong recovery choice
to omit evidence only when it is the scene's sole non-retry exit; strong choices
with genuine alternatives still have to record evidence.

A seeded path test also walks Mission 5 with weak reasoning and a coached voice
retry, then confirms the finished attempt remains developing rather than being
upgraded to demonstrated.

## Build verification

The default Turbopack build initially failed inside the Codex command

  Both builders produce all 28 routes; the default Turbopack build now passes
  in both review environments.
- **Data and niche:** no schema, logging, network or input change. Student

## Deliberately left

- The scene still includes a missing shadow and a reversed playground detail.
  Removing them would make the distinction impossible to practise. They now do
  the right instructional job: trigger a pause without claiming certainty.
- The fictional penguin image is never forensically proved generated. The class
  establishes that its claims conflict with witnesses and that nobody can trace
  its source, which is enough to stop sharing without pretending to know more.
- The teacher board feedback panel reaches the fixed control bar at 1280×800,
  but no text or action is covered. A layout change would add churn without
  improving this content decision.
- Claude's review found the same forced-answer evidence pattern in eight other
  missions. Correcting that system-wide is the next integrity sprint: it needs
  an explicit product rule and migration/seed review, not an unnoticed change
  hidden inside this one mission's content edit.
