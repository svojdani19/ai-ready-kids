# Sprint 18 review — 27 August 2026

**Scope.** Two findings on original verification missions. Both correct. Both
are the same class of defect and the sharpest yet: the mission asserts a
conclusion its own story has not earned, and then awards mastery for repeating
it.

Four of the original nine have now had a proper read. Four had findings.

---

## Finding 1 — "The Penguin on the Playground" never established that the picture was fake

The mission's whole thesis is that provenance beats inspection, and it said so
in its guide. Then it did the opposite.

The circulating photo carried **no date and no claimed capture time**. Despite
that, *"It did not snow yesterday and you were outside all afternoon"* earned
`demonstrated`. First-hand knowledge of yesterday cannot settle an undated
image; nothing said the picture was from yesterday. The child was reasoning from
an assumed context, which is precisely the move the mission exists to prevent.

Ms. Okafor was then asked **only about the voice message**. That established the
audio was false. The mission treated it as having established the picture was
too: the strong action became *"tell the people you got it from that it is not
real"*, singular, conflating two artefacts that had been checked to completely
different depths. The ending and the guide both spoke of the picture as a
confirmed fake.

So the mission acted out its own error. It reached a verdict from appearance and
assumed context, having traced nothing.

### What changed

**The image now carries a claim.** Somebody has typed under it: *PENGUIN AT
BRIGHTWOOD THIS MORNING!!!* Now there is something to test, and the snow check
is a real one — and its feedback is careful about what it bought: *"That settles
the words underneath the picture. Where the picture itself came from is still
nobody."*

**The voice check gained its durable half.** Asking Ms. Okafor is scoped to what
it can bear — *"For a homework message from your own teacher, standing in front
of her settles it, because she is the person it is supposed to have come from"*
— rather than standing as a general law that a subject's denial disproves a
recording. The second full-credit move is the provenance one: **check the class
page, where homework actually gets posted.** That also gave the scene a second
exit, so it can record evidence honestly instead of relying on the sole-exit
exemption sprint 06 introduced for it.

**The correction now says exactly what was found.** The full-credit answer is
two sentences: *"Ms. Okafor did not send the homework message. Nobody knows
where the picture came from."* Telling people the whole thing is fake is
`partial`: *"Calling it fake is a guess, and it is the same mistake as believing
it, pointing the other way."*

**The picture is never resolved.** Nobody traces it. Ms. Okafor writes UNKNOWN
beside it on the board and leaves it up all week, "which Theo finds far more
annoying than an answer would have been." New wrap-up line: **say exactly what
you found out; unknown is a real answer and it beats a guess.**

## Finding 2 — "Two Answers, One Truth" was built on a conflict that does not exist

AskMe says the school was built in 1908. The plaque says ERECTED 1961. The
mission said: *"They cannot both be right."*

They can. A school can open in 1908 and move into a building put up in 1961.
Nothing in the mission defined whether the question was the founding of the
institution or the construction of the current building — and *"the year the
school was built"* is two questions wearing one coat.

On top of that it claimed the plaque **"was put there by the people who built
it"** and was therefore first-hand. A plaque establishes neither. They get
installed years later, by people who were not present, saying whatever narrow
thing somebody chose to engrave. Scene five then generalised all of this into a
league table — *the thing that was there* outranks records — and the wrap-up
made it a law: **"First-hand beats repeated. The plaque beats the blog."**

The mission was rewarding deference to an official-looking object.

### What changed

**Nobody is wrong, and that is the lesson.** Scene four no longer asks which one
to believe. It asks what is worth noticing first, and the two full-credit
answers are *the plaque says ERECTED, that is about a building* and *they might
both be true, about two different things*. Asking which year the poster wants is
`partial` — a fair move, and the words are already in front of you.

**"The plaque wins. It is old and official and it is right there" is the
retry**, with a coach note saying this deference is the failure the mission is
really about and is more common than believing the app.

**The district building records settle it**, because that is the record whose
job it is: Brightwood opened in 1908 on Cedar Street; the present building went
up in 1961 after a fire. *"The disagreement was never between them, it was
inside your question."* Picking the plaque as the loser is the retry there —
*"You are still trying to pick a winner."*

The source hierarchy is gone. The wrap-up is now: work out what the question is
before deciding which answer is wrong; read what a source actually says, because
erected is not the same as founded; two answers that disagree might be answering
two different questions; find the record whose job it is to keep that fact.

The badge changed from Source Finder to **Question Sorter**, which is what the
mission now teaches.

## Guards added

Six tests, plus five phrases banned across the whole curriculum:
`first-hand beats repeated`, `the plaque beats the blog`,
`put there by the people who built it`, `they cannot both be right`,
`asking the real person is the check`.

Two existing tests had to be rewritten rather than extended, and both were
pinning something that had become wrong:

- The penguin voice scene was asserted to have exactly one exit recording no
  evidence — the sprint 06 workaround. It now has two real checks and records
  evidence, so the assertion was pinning a limitation as though it were a
  design.
- The coached-completion test asserted an exact choice-id path through a scene
  this sprint restructured. Re-pointed at the outcome it actually cares about:
  a weak learner comes out `developing` on the mission's primary skill.

**309 tests, up from 303.**

## What this says about the review process

Both findings are the shape sprint 13 added to the checklist — *the ending does
not act out the error* — and neither was caught by it, because I had only ever
applied that item to missions I was already reading for something else. The
original nine have never been swept for it.

The penguin mission is the more instructive failure. Its guide states the
correct principle in plain words: "the durable moves are asking who made it, who
witnessed it". A reader checking the guide against the checklist would have
passed it. The defect was that the *story* never did what the guide said, and
the evidence awards followed the story. **Guides describe intent; scenes are
what a child actually does.** Check the scenes.

- Teacher-facing copy still promises timelines it cannot support.
