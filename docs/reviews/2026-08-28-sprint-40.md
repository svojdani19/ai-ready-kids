# Sprint 40 — the last real-classroom photograph, and the wrong lesson with it

**Date:** 2026-08-28
**Scope:** one P1 finding. The extension for `what-the-camera-sees`. This closes
the defect class sprint 37 opened.

## The finding

The extension read:

> Before school, photograph your empty classroom from where a tablet usually
> sits. Project that picture — never a live feed, and never a fresh photo with
> children in it — and have the class list everything a stranger could learn
> from it. Take a second empty-room picture after they tidy the walls and
> compare the lists.

Two problems, and the second is the more interesting one.

**"Empty" and "before school" remove faces, and nothing else.** Names on work,
reading and support groups, collection arrangements, timetables, accommodation
notices and school identifiers are all still on the walls at 7am — an empty room
is exactly the state in which the paperwork is most visible. It still required
sourcing, prep and media-policy judgement, and a projected image can be
re-photographed. The care in the wording was real, and it was aimed at the wrong
risk.

**It taught the wrong skill.** Compare a messy wall with a tidy one and the
lesson a class draws is *tidy the wall*. But this mission's four learning goals
are about telling a preview from a live stream **by reading what the screen
says**, understanding that once live each new moment goes out before it can be
checked, and turning a feature off rather than managing it. A tidy live frame is
still a frame nobody can check in advance. The activity was quietly teaching the
control the mission exists to say you do not have.

## What changed

Three authored screens, zero prep, on the existing `ExtensionCard` path. The
room is fictional and domestic, matching the mission's own setting (Star Barn,
a bedroom), and the labels are the mission's own strings verbatim.

| | Screen | Status label | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | A room corner: school jumper with the badge showing, a street sign readable through the window, a birthday card with a name, and a band poster | `PREVIEW — ONLY YOU CAN SEE THIS` | Graded clues. The badge and the sign are the strong ones; the poster says nothing about where anybody is. Nobody has received any of it — **and only the label can tell you that**. |
| **2** | The same room tidied. Jumper away, card off the shelf, blind down. Plain wall. | `PREVIEW — ONLY YOU CAN SEE THIS` | The frame is genuinely clean, **the words did not change when the room did**, and tidying fixed this one frame and not the next one. |
| **3** | The same wall, the same angle, the same everything. **The room has not changed at all.** | `LIVE — 8 PLAYERS CAN SEE THIS NOW` | **The words changed and the picture did not.** The next moment is not promised: the door could open a second from now, and because the label says LIVE that moment is on eight screens before it is on this one. |

Screens 2 and 3 are the pair. The frame is identical, so a child cannot answer
by looking harder at the room — the only thing that moved is the status line.
That is the habit the mission wants and the old activity trained against. The
door that could open belongs to the next-moment prompt, never to the picture:
it is the little brother from scene `s3` as a thing that has not happened yet,
so the activity and the story teach the same beat without giving a child a
visible change to blame.

**Four prompts, kept apart**, one per card and named in the instructions: what
the picture gives away, what the words prove about who is receiving it *right
now*, why tidying cannot fix the next moment, and whether turning the feature
off is the safer choice. The four map onto the mission's four learning goals.

Hand signals, nothing written down about any child. The prohibition is explicit
and covers the two ways a teacher might improvise:

> Do not point a camera at anything, do not use a photo of your own room, your
> school, your students or their families, and do not open a live feature to
> demonstrate one — these three screens exist so that you never have to, and a
> picture on a projector can be photographed off the wall.

**Timing.** 635 words, about five minutes read aloud, leaving five for
discussion. No device required.

## Implementation

One optional field on the existing `ExtensionCard`: `control?`, rendered as
"The next moment:". No new format, no second renderer, both teacher surfaces
pick it up. It sits alongside `consent?` from sprint 38; each is documented as
applying only where a mission teaches that dimension, and a test asserts the
split rather than leaving it to convention.

## The guard is now empty

The set of missions whose extension asks a teacher to photograph their own
classroom has gone `["the-class-photo", "what-the-camera-sees"]` → `["what-the-
camera-sees"]` → `[]`. The test now asserts the empty set, so no mission can
reintroduce the instruction.

## Tests

Nine, all confirmed to **fail against the old extension and pass against the
new** by stashing the content and re-running: no camera, no photograph and no
live feature; the real-photo prohibition with its reason; the mission's own
`PREVIEW`/`LIVE` strings on the right cards and every `proves` line about the
label rather than the room; the same-frame status change, including that the
picture explicitly has not changed; tidy-not-sufficient asserted on the card
where the room is tidy; the four questions named and kept apart; graded clues
with a deliberately harmless item; and the empty guard.

One older assertion was superseded and rewritten rather than deleted. It pinned
the previous mitigations — *never a live feed*, *never a fresh photo with
children in it*, *prepared image* — which were careful handling of a photograph
that no longer exists. It now asserts that no camera is involved at all, which
is the stronger property.

485 tests, up from 478.

## Verified in the browser

Both teacher surfaces at **1280×800** and **768×1024**: no horizontal overflow,
all three cards inside the content column, nothing clipped, and **four
definition rows per card at every size**, confirming the new "The next moment"
line renders everywhere. Card widths 702 / 662 px on the guide, 442 / 686 px on
the mission page.

## Deliberately not changed

Scenes, mission graph, evidence, student fields, the authored-choice design and
every standing constraint. Guide content only.

## The defect class, closed

Three missions had it, and they graded neatly by how much the author was
thinking about the hazard:

- **The Filter That Wanted More** — asked for photographs, left the contents to
  chance.
- **The Class Photo** — named the sensitive material as the target, which read
  as rigour.
- **What the Camera Sees** — mitigated the photograph carefully and still asked
  for one, *and* the mitigation ("empty", "tidy the walls") pulled the lesson
  toward the wrong skill.

That last one is the lesson worth keeping. **Careful handling of a hazard can
quietly become the curriculum.** The safety wording here was not decoration; it
shaped what the activity taught, and what it taught was that tidying works. When
a mitigation has to be explained to students, check whether the explanation has
replaced the lesson.

## Sprint 41 correction

Screen 3 claimed *"same wall, same angle, same everything"* and *"the picture
has not changed at all"* while its description added a door beginning to open at
the edge of the frame. The regression test asserted **both** at once, so it
locked the contradiction in rather than catching it.

An opening door is a visible change. A seven-year-old can therefore attribute
the risk to the thing that moved and never reach the actual lesson: the frame is
identical, and the `LIVE` label is the only evidence that eight people are
receiving it.

Screen 3's visible frame is now exactly screen 2's — clean, nothing moving,
nothing entering. *"The room has not changed at all. One difference, and it is
in the words underneath."* The door moved to the next-moment line as a
hypothetical:

> This frame is fine, and the next one is not promised. The door could open a
> second from now, or somebody could walk in, and because the label says LIVE
> that moment is on eight screens before it is on this one. A live moment cannot
> be checked first. Checking is something you do to a preview.

The test now enforces the separation from both sides: screen 3's description and
`suggests` may contain no door, no opening, nobody entering and nothing starting
or beginning; and the `control` line must contain the possible door opening, the
one-second framing, and the cannot-check-first rule. 486 tests.
