# Sprint 47 — the board covered the application but not the tab order

**Date:** 2026-08-28
**Scope:** Classroom Mode. Modal semantics, focus containment, and recovery on
Exit.

## The finding

Classroom Mode renders `fixed inset-0` over the teacher application. Until now
that was all it did:

- it was **not announced as a modal region**, so assistive technology described
  it as one more part of the page behind it;
- it **did not contain focus**, so the teacher header, sidebar and plan page
  underneath stayed in the tab order behind an opaque board;
- its **Exit button unmounted while focused** and returned to the plan without
  restoring focus to the launcher.

The failure mode is specific and it happens in front of a class. A teacher
driving the lesson from the keyboard tabs one stop too far and lands on a
control the projector is covering. **Nothing on screen changes**, because the
focused element is behind the board — so there is no way to tell what happened
or how far they have gone. Then Exit drops them at the top of the document while
twenty-three children wait.

This is the third instalment of the same defect class as sprints 45 and 46, and
the worst of them: the earlier two were a marketing menu, this one is the thing
a teacher is holding while a room watches.

## What changed

**Both full-screen states are one labelled modal surface.** Present and debrief
each carry `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal="true"` and a name — *"Sprocket Wants to
Know on the board"* and *"… on the board — talk about it"*. To the room they are
one thing, the board, and they are described that way. **The plan page is not
modal** and gains nothing; reading a lesson plan is ordinary page reading.

**Tab and Shift+Tab stay inside the surface.** `useFocusTrap` intercepts Tab,
collects the focusable stops inside the surface and wraps at both ends. Focus
sitting on the stage wrapper — or anywhere outside — enters the cycle at
whichever end the direction implies.

Two deliberate choices:

- **Tab interception rather than making the background inert.** It keeps the
  change inside Classroom Mode, as scoped, and reaches nothing the component
  does not own.
- **`tabindex="-1"` is excluded from the cycle.** The stage wrapper carries it so
  scene changes can move focus to the teaching content programmatically; it
  should not become a stop a teacher has to tab past.

**It contains without trapping.** Exit, ← Back, Next/Go to debrief and Notes are
all on screen and all in the cycle, which is the only thing that makes
containment defensible. A test asserts they are visible.

**Exit returns focus to the exact launcher that opened the board.** There are two
"Put it on the board" buttons — one in the sticky plan header, one at the foot of
the plan — and which one was pressed is recorded at launch. A teacher who
launched from the bottom of the plan and lands back at the top has lost their
place, which is the same defect in a smaller form.

**Only Exit restores.** The flag is set by Exit alone, so *"Finish and open the
guide"* navigates normally and nothing reaches in to pull focus back.

**The presenter shortcuts are untouched.** Arrows, space, Page Up/Down, 1–4, N,
and Escape still returning from a revealed branch to the choice list. **Escape is
not an exit from Classroom Mode** and the trap ignores it entirely.

### An implementation note

Both the launch origin and the restore flag are `useRef`, not state. Neither is
rendered, and the effect that reads them already re-runs on the stage change that
matters. The first draft used state and tripped
`react-hooks/set-state-in-effect` — correctly, because clearing a flag inside an
effect is a cascading render for something the UI never displays.

## Tests

Eleven added to `tests/classroom-mode.test.tsx`, twenty-two in the file. **Nine
confirmed to fail without the fix** by stashing the component.

They render the real component **inside the kind of page furniture it covers** —
a header link, a sign-out button, a footer link — so "does Tab reach the
navigation" is a question the test can actually ask rather than assume.

- modal semantics on present and debrief, and **no dialog on the plan page**;
- forward containment and reverse containment, thirty stops each, asserting on
  every iteration that focus is inside the surface **and** that each underlying
  control does not have it;
- debrief containment;
- the visible exits are present, so this is containment and not a trap;
- exact-launcher recovery from **both** launch buttons;
- no focus theft when the debrief navigates to the guide;
- a stage change moves focus to something inside the surface and visible;
- a shortcut regression covering 1–4, Escape returning to the choice list **with
  the dialog still open**, N toggling notes both ways, and arrows/Page keys
  paging the running order forwards and back.

**542 tests, up from 531.**

## Verified in the browser

**1280×800, keyboard only.** Launched from the **second** button. Surface reports
`role="dialog"`, `aria-modal="true"`, the right label; the plan page has no
dialog. 30 Tabs and 25 Shift+Tabs later focus is still inside the board, on
*Next →* and *Exit* respectively. Driven to the debrief with ArrowRight alone:
label switches to *"— talk about it"*, still modal, 12 more Tabs stay contained.
← Back then Exit → dialog gone, **focus on the second launcher**, visible.

**768×1024.** Same containment over 20 Tabs. All four board controls are 44px
tall, no horizontal overflow, so projector and touch use are unaffected. Exit
from a header launch returned focus to the **header** launcher, visible.

## Out of scope, as instructed

No curriculum content, no recording behaviour, no student data, no subscription
behaviour, and no change to what Escape means.

## Observed, not changed

**The background is not `inert`.** Containment is enforced by intercepting Tab,
so the covered controls remain focusable in principle — a screen-reader virtual
cursor or a browser's "find all links" could still surface them, and
`aria-modal="true"` is the only thing telling assistive technology to ignore
them. Real `inert` on the background would be stronger, and it means reaching
outside this component. Worth doing deliberately rather than as a side effect
here.

## The lesson

**Covering the screen is a visual act; the tab order does not know about
paint.** `fixed inset-0` with a high `z-index` makes something look like the
only thing on screen, and every keyboard user still has the whole page behind it.
That gap is invisible to the person who built it — you cannot see focus land on
something a board is covering — which is why it survived a working product and
two previous accessibility sprints.

The pattern across sprints 45, 46 and 47 is now clear enough to name: **this
product builds overlays well and finishes them for pointers.** The remaining
candidates are already listed in `LATEST.md`.
