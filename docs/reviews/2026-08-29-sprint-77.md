# Sprint 77 — a teacher's guide to running a session

**Date:** 2026-08-29
**Reviewed against:** HEAD `a12032a`
**Scope:** authored teacher content and one route. No child data model change,
no new student fields, no telemetry, no live model, no product broadening.

## The gap

Every session already shipped a discussion guide: what it teaches, what to watch
for, the debrief questions, the answers to what children predictably say, and an
unplugged extension. None of it told a teacher **how to run the twenty minutes** —
when children are on devices and when they are not, what the adult does while
they play, or what to do when eight of them finish four minutes before the rest.

The printable guide header has asserted *"N minutes independent, 15 minutes
debrief"* for many sprints. **Nothing anywhere described that shape.**

## What was built

`src/content/session-guide.ts` — one authored source, read by three surfaces so
they cannot drift:

- **Two session shapes**, because the product has two kinds of session and they
  are taught differently. A **First Look** session is led from the board (3 / 10
  / 5 / 5 minutes) and needs no device at all; a **core mission** is played
  independently and debriefed (3 / 7–9 / 3 / 15). Each step says what *you* do
  and what *they* do.
- **Room setups** — one device each, a few devices on rotation, or one board and
  none. The rotation entry names the trap: do not debrief the first group in
  front of the group that has not played, because that hands them the answer.
- **When the room does what rooms do** — the child who finishes in four minutes,
  the one asking you what to pick, the one upset by a situation, the one who
  wants to redo a finished mission, and the class that wants to argue.
- **Five things not to do**, each of which undoes a deliberate product decision:
  telling the class the safe choice first, reading a child's choices to the room,
  treating a rethink as wrong, adding a timer or points chart, or setting an
  extension that asks children to write something about themselves.
- **What each kind of session leaves behind**, written from the code.

Surfaces: `/teacher/how-to-run-a-session` (new, in the teacher nav and linked
from the overview); a **run sheet** on each mission page and on the **printable**
guide, using that session's own `estimatedMinutes` rather than a generic figure,
and picking the shape from the session's own `segment`.

So the printed header's claim is now explained directly beneath it.

## A false claim the guide exposed

Writing "what each session records" meant checking rather than assuming. The
educator orientation said:

> First Look records nothing on the roster, deliberately.

Traced in code — assign a First Look session, play it to the end, rebuild the
report:

| | Result |
| --- | --- |
| attempt row created | **yes** |
| counts toward `missionsCompleted` / completion rate | **yes** |
| moves any competency figure | **no** |

So the claim was wrong in its first half and right in its second. Corrected to
say what is true: First Look records **no skill evidence**, so no competency
figure moves and no competency total includes it — while it *does* record that
the child opened and finished the session, which counts as completed work; and a
First Look run on the board with nobody signed in records nothing about any
individual at all.

The guide could not have been written accurately without fixing this, which is
why it is in scope rather than deferred.

## Known gaps

- **The timings are authored judgements, not measured.** 3/9/3/15 is a defensible
  shape for a 25–30 minute slot; nobody has timed it in a real classroom, and the
  page does not claim anyone has.
- **No evidence base is claimed.** The guidance says what the product's design
  implies and what an experienced teacher would recognise. It cites no research,
  because none was consulted.
- **First Look is assignable and counted like a core mission.** This sprint
  corrected the *description* of that; whether a First Look session should count
  toward the completion rate at all is a product question that remains open.
- **The run sheet substitutes minutes by step index** (`i === 1` for the
  independent-play step). If a shape gains or reorders steps, that index goes
  stale silently — a named step id would be sturdier.
