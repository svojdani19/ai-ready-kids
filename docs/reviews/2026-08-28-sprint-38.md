# Sprint 38 — The Class Photo taught consent by exposing a real room

**Date:** 2026-08-28
**Scope:** one P1 supporting-content finding, confirmed from sprint 37's own
"found but not fixed" note. One mission's extension.

## The finding

`the-class-photo`'s extension read:

> Take a photo of your own classroom, project it, and have the class hunt the
> background for anything they would not publish. Names on trays, timetables, a
> rota with home details. They will find more than you expect.

This is more direct than sprint 37's defect. That one asked for photographs and
left it to chance what was in them; this one **named the sensitive material as
the target**. In a mission about consent and limited audiences, it put a real
room's actual student and routine information on a shared screen and set
twenty-odd children hunting through it.

What that can surface, in the room, in front of everybody: a family-restricted
child's arrangements, who collects whom, a support or intervention timetable, a
reading-group placement. It needs media-policy judgement and preparation, and a
projected screen can be photographed off the wall. And it modelled that privacy
is taught by displaying the very data children are being told to protect.

## What changed

Three authored cards, reusing sprint 37's `ExtensionCard` structure and
renderer. They are **staged**, and the order is the lesson — this mission's rule
has three parts and the cards walk all three:

| | Card | Stage |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Room 9's photo with a readable chart behind them: reading groups with names, and a column headed `WHO GOES HOME WITH WHO` | **Background, and consent still pending.** Not ready for two reasons: the chart alone stops this version, and taking it down is necessary but not sufficient — after the retake all twenty-three still have to be asked about the exact named audience. |
| **2** | The same class, next morning, chart gone, plain brick wall. All twenty-three asked one at a time about the school news page. All twenty-three said yes. | **Everyone's own yes.** Ready — for the school news page, because that is the exact thing they were asked about. |
| **3** | That same clean photo, proposed for a cousin who is not at the school and a group chat of about forty | **Audience.** Not ready. Twenty-three yeses, every one to the school news page. |

Photo 3 is the one that matters. **Nothing about the picture changes between 2
and 3, and the answer changes anyway** — so a child cannot resolve it by looking
harder at the image, which is precisely the move this mission exists to
interrupt. It lands directly on two misconceptions the guide already lists:
*"It is already going online, so sending it changes nothing"* and *"Ravi is not
in this one, so I can send it."*

Card 2 says **"asked, one at a time, and answered"** and explicitly **"Not
nobody objected"** — the distinction the whole mission turns on, and one that is
easy to lose.

Each card answers four things kept apart: what the background *suggests*, what
it *proves*, whether **everyone in it** was asked, and whether it is ready for
**this** audience.

The room answers by hand signal — thumb up, flat hand, thumb down — and the text
says nothing is written down about any child. The prohibition names the old
target directly:

> Do not use a photo of your own room, your students, their families, or your
> school's real charts, lists and timetables — these three exist so that you
> never have to, and a picture on a projector can be photographed off the wall.

**Timing.** 502 words, about four minutes read aloud, leaving roughly six for
discussion across three cards. Works with no device at all.

## Implementation

One optional field added to the existing `ExtensionCard`: `consent?: string`,
rendered as "Everyone in it:" between Proves and the audience line. It is
optional because it only applies where a mission teaches consent as a step
separate from background — The Class Photo does, The Filter That Wanted More
does not, and a test asserts exactly that split. No new format, no second
renderer; both teacher surfaces pick it up.

## Tests

Six new, all confirmed to **fail against the old extension and pass against the
new** by stashing the content and re-running. They cover the three stages
individually — chart-then-nobody-asked, clean-and-everybody-said-yes-to-a-named-
destination, same-photo-wrong-audience — plus the no-real-photo rule with its
reason, the three questions being named and kept apart, hand signals with
nothing recorded, and distinctness from the Filter cards.

**The guard has shrunk as required.** The set of missions whose extension asks
for a real classroom photograph is now exactly `["what-the-camera-sees"]`, down
from two. It may shrink further; nothing may join it.

477 tests, up from 471.

## Verified in the browser

Both teacher surfaces — the printable discussion guide and the mission detail
page — at **1280×800** and **768×1024**. No horizontal overflow, all three cards
inside the content column, nothing clipped, and **four definition rows per card
at every size**, confirming the new consent line renders everywhere. Card widths
702 / 662 px on the guide, 442 / 686 px on the mission page.

## Still outstanding in this defect class

`what-the-camera-sees` is the remaining case, and it is the mitigated one: an
empty room, photographed before school, never a live feed, never children in
frame, a prepared image offered as an alternative, and "nothing in this activity
turns a camera on in front of the class." It still asks a teacher to photograph
a real school interior, which is still sourcing, still prep, and still a real
building on a projector. It is a smaller problem than the two now fixed, and it
is the last one the guard knows about.

**The other twenty-four guides have not been read for this defect class.** Three
of the first three checked had some version of it. That base rate has not
improved.

## Deliberately not changed

- **The mission graph, evidence, scenes, student fields.** Guide content only.
- **The family take-home**, which concerns a family's own photos on their own
  device.
- **No unrelated guides touched.**

## Sprint 39 correction

Photo 1's audience line originally read *"Not ready, and not because of anybody
in it. Take the chart down and take the picture again."* That was written to
mean "the chart is what blocks **this** version" — but to a seven-year-old it
says the unasked children do not matter, and it makes the retake sound like the
whole fix. It contradicted the mission's own three-part rule inside the card
meant to introduce it.

Corrected so both blockers stay live, in child-friendly sequence:

> Not ready, for two reasons. The chart alone stops this version, so take it
> down and photograph them again. That fixes the wall and it is not the whole
> job: after the retake, all twenty-three still have to be asked about the exact
> place it is going.

The consent line now ties to the chart rather than floating free of it:
*"Nobody has been asked yet. That question does not go away when the chart comes
down."* The dismissive phrasing is removed from the content and from this review
and `LATEST.md`, and a regression test asserts that Photo 1's audience line
names **both** blockers, says necessary-but-not-sufficient in as many words,
points at the exact audience, and that the phrase appears on no card in any
mission. 478 tests.

## The lesson

Sprint 37's lesson was that an activity teaching restraint must not require the
thing it warns about. This one sharpens it: **the strongest version of the
defect looks like the most thorough version of the lesson.** "Names on trays,
timetables, a rota with home details" reads as rigour — the author naming
exactly what to look for — and it is the sentence that turns the activity into
the exposure. Specificity about the hazard is not the same as safety from it,
and here the specificity was the delivery mechanism.
