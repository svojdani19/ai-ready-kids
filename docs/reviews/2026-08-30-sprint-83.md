# Sprint 83 — the guide promised families evidence the page cannot hold

**Date:** 2026-08-30
**Reviewed against:** HEAD `a814294`
**Scope:** teacher-facing copy in `src/content/session-guide.ts`, plus one new
test file. No route changed, no component changed, no schema, no field, no data
flow. `/family/[slug]` is byte-for-byte what it was.

One unrelated assertion in `tests/confirm-action.test.tsx` was also corrected,
because the gate failed intermittently while landing this and an unreliable gate
outranks the sprint it is blocking. It is a test-timing fix, not a product
change; the section below says what it was.

## The finding

`WHAT_IS_RECORDED` told teachers what a core mission leaves behind:

> Which authored choices the child tapped, and a per-skill judgment of
> demonstrated or developing worked out from those choices. **That is the
> evidence on your roster and in the family take-home.**

`NOT_THIS` said the same thing from the other side — a child's evidence is *"for
you and their family"*.

Neither is true of the page. `/family/[slug]` is one statically generated page
per mission: prerendered at build time, no session, no child id, no record lookup
of any kind. It renders four authored fields — `family.summary`,
`family.questions`, `family.tryAtHome`, `family.familyRule` — and it renders the
same four for every family in the class. It could not carry an individual result
if it wanted to.

This was always loose. Sprint 81's acceptance correction is what made it plainly
contradictory: that correction went through the buyer-facing copy and said
outright that the take-home is a **free public resource**, generic, with nothing
behind it to sign into. The product now describes that page accurately in five
places and inaccurately in the one place a teacher reads before standing in front
of a class.

**Why that ordering matters.** A teacher repeats what this guide says. The damage
is not a stale sentence in a file; it is a caregiver told to look for their
child's result and finding generic curriculum, a seven-to-ten-year-old told their
family will see how they did, and a buyer reading the product's own explanation
of its data and finding it promise child evidence on a page that cannot
technically contain it. Two of those three are trust in a classroom. The third is
the privacy story, which is this product's main commercial argument.

## The correction

Copy only, in one file.

**The core-mission row now ends where the evidence ends:**

> Which authored choices the child tapped, and a per-skill judgment of
> demonstrated or developing worked out from those choices. That evidence lives
> in one place: your roster and dashboard. It is not sent anywhere and no family
> page shows it.

**The take-home is its own row**, rather than being appended to a sentence about
evidence — because "what does this record" is a question it deserves answered
directly:

> **The family take-home** — Nothing about any child. It is the same printed page
> for every family in the class — what the mission practiced, three questions to
> ask, one thing to try, and the rule worth keeping. If you want a family to know
> how their own child did, that comes from you, the way it always has.

That last sentence is doing deliberate work. Having removed a channel a teacher
believed existed, the guide has to say what replaces it, and the honest answer is
that nothing does and nothing needs to: a teacher tells a family how their child
is doing the way they always have. Inventing a parent account or a messaging
channel to fill the gap would have been the wrong correction and a new data flow
in a product built to have as few as possible.

**`NOT_THIS` was reconciled**, not merely trimmed. The old line implied the family
receives the judgment; the new one keeps the real instruction and adds the guard
the finding exposes:

> Do not read a child's choices out to the room, or compare two children's
> screens. What a child chose is for you to teach from, not for an audience.
>
> Do not tell a child or a caregiver to look for their own result on the
> take-home sheet. It is the same page for the whole class and holds no
> individual result — promising one there is a promise the page cannot keep.

## The gate failed twice while landing this, and that is reported rather than re-run

The first full run after the copy change failed one test in
`tests/checkin-player.test.tsx` — a file this sprint does not touch. **I did not
capture its error text**: the command filtered output to summary lines and threw
the message away, which is the exact mistake sprint 79 was written about, made in
a new way. It has not recurred in eleven subsequent full runs, and I cannot say
what it was. It is recorded below as observed-once and undiagnosed, not as flake.

Running again with output kept produced a **different** failure, in
`tests/confirm-action.test.tsx`, and this one was captured in full:

```
FAIL tests/confirm-action.test.tsx > collapses on an expected error,
     announces it, and returns focus for recovery
Error: expect(element).toHaveFocus()
Expected element with focus: <button>Delete data</button>
Received element with focus: <body>
```

**Diagnosed, not guessed.** The test did `await waitFor(() =>
expect(launcher()).toBeInTheDocument())` and then read focus synchronously. The
launcher returns to the document one commit *before* the effect that focuses it
runs, so `waitFor` resolved in the gap and the assertion read `<body>`. The
product is not at fault: `ConfirmAction` sets `restoreFocus` before collapsing and
guards against focusing a detached node, and in a browser the effect commits
before anyone could look.

The fix awaits the outcome rather than the element — `await waitFor(() =>
expect(launcher()).toHaveFocus())` — which asserts the same thing and is not a
loosened assertion. **Mutation-checked:** deleting `el.focus()` from the component
fails 4 tests including this one, so it still catches a real focus regression.

Eleven consecutive full runs since, all 946 green.

## Known gaps

- **The vocabulary is a denylist.** Nine phrasings of "individual result" are
  pinned. A tenth, worded differently, would pass. The route half of the coupling
  is structural and the copy half is not, which is the same shape sprint 81's
  correction ended on.
- **Only two files are swept by test.** The assertions cover `session-guide.ts`
  and the family route. The eight other surfaces in the table above were read by
  hand this sprint and nothing stops one of them acquiring the claim tomorrow.
- **"That comes from you, the way it always has" describes a practice, not a
  feature.** It is true and it is the right answer, but the product does not help
  with it at all, and a teacher who wanted to send a family something specific
  still has nothing but their own words.
- **Nothing checks the guide against the roster page.** The claim that evidence
  lives on "your roster and dashboard" is asserted as copy, not verified against
  what those pages actually render.
- **One intermittent failure is still undiagnosed.** `checkin-player.test.tsx >
  restores the saved selection when going Back` failed once and I did not keep
  the error. It has not returned in eleven runs. It is the same *shape* as the
  one that was fixed — a synchronous assertion after an async settle — but that
  is a hypothesis, and a hypothesis is what sprint 79 said not to act on.
- **Nothing stops the next assertion racing a commit.** The two found here were
  found by the suite failing, not by a rule. Component tests that read focus or
  state immediately after `waitFor` on a *different* condition are a pattern, and
  nothing enumerates them.
