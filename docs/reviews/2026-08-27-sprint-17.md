# Sprint 17 review — 27 August 2026

**Scope.** Two findings on the first two of the original nine missions. Both
correct. The second asked for the fix to be applied as a sweep to all eight
legacy forced-award scenes rather than pinned to the one that was reported, so
that is what this sprint does. That item has been open since sprint 06.

Two further defects were found by the fixes themselves: a fourth instance of
sprint 16's public-correction problem, missed because that guard was written
too literally, and a rendering defect the browser check surfaced.

## Finding 1 — the eight forced-award scenes

**Sprocket Wants to Know**, scene six, asked which of three things is a private
fact. Favourite colour and dinosaurs both looped back with feedback telling the
child to pick the one that helps somebody find them. **The street you live on**
was the only way out, and it recorded `privacy.identity` demonstrated.

Every child who finished that scene took that choice. So the record said they
had completed the scene, not that they could tell a private fact from a public
one — and a teacher reading the dashboard could not tell the difference. It also
reduced a contextual skill to a one-answer recall check.

Seven more scenes had the same shape, across five missions.

### The remedy, applied as a rule rather than eight times

The sweep lives in `validateMission`, which every mission is checked against:

> If a scene has exactly one non-retry exit, that exit must not record
> evidence. Either give the scene a second plausible way out, or award nothing.

That is the mirror of a rule already there — a sole exit was allowed to *omit*
evidence precisely because it cannot distinguish reasoning from compliance. The
missing half said it must. Adding it turned six missions red, which is the
correct number.

Each scene then got a second genuinely correct exit, preferring a contextual
distinction over another obvious answer:

- **Sprocket / s6** now asks: *Sprocket already knows your first name. Which of
  these would you not add to it?* Both **which school you go to** and **the
  street you live on** are independently correct, for stated and different
  reasons — a school is a building and a time, a street is nearly a doorstep.
  Favourite colour is gone; dinosaurs remains as the thing to get wrong.
- **The Question at Bedtime / s4** gained *"It stopped being about my homework
  and started being about me"* — which the mission's own `lookFor` already
  called out as a thing to watch for, and which was sitting behind a retry.
- **The Very Sure Answer / s3**: *"it said researchers confirmed it"* is a
  reason-shaped answer — the child went looking for a source — and is now a
  `partial` exit rather than a loop, because that instinct is better than
  "computers do not make mistakes" and should not be scored the same.
- **The Very Sure Answer / s7** was a fill-in-the-blank with one answer. It now
  says *more than one thing fits*, and **having checked** joins **being right**.
- **The Homework That Did Itself / s6** gained *"good help leaves me able to do
  the next one on my own"* — the same test pointed forwards.
- **Four Doors / s2**: asking a person for seven plus eight is slower, not
  wrong. It is a `partial` exit now.
- **Four Doors / s6** gained *"and the same problem can need a different door
  next week"*.
- **The Spelling Test Surprise / s3** gained *"the tablet did the spelling, so
  my hand never learned it"*.

The coaching rule from sprint 10 does the rest: reaching a correct exit after a
retry records `developing`. Sixteen new assertions check both halves per scene —
demonstrated when chosen first, developing when reached after a correction —
through the real `recordDecision`, scoped to the scene rather than the mission
because a skill demonstrated independently elsewhere legitimately stays
demonstrated.

## Finding 2 — The Filter That Wanted More taught minimisation without approval

The mission went straight to the permission box. A child on a school tablet,
with an app neither of them had heard of, exposing a feed with 2,411 people on
it — and *"a fox filter only needs the camera"* earned mastery. A strange app can
need the camera to function perfectly well and still be one nobody has approved.

**Approval now comes first, in its own scene, before any permission is
granted.** Two full-credit answers — *is this one allowed on a school tablet?*
and *let us go and ask Ms. Okafor* — and reading the permission list is the
`partial`: "a good question, and it is the second one". *Theo already has it, so
it must be fine* is the retry, with a coach note saying why that is the
strongest permission signal at this age and the least reliable.

Ms. Okafor settles it: Dazzle is on the allowed list for making pictures, and
nothing gets posted from a school tablet. The minimisation lesson then runs
exactly as before, on an app that has actually been approved.

**The posting decision no longer turns on the background.** Declining because
the school sign is visible implied the same photo would be fine on an open feed
with a clean wall behind it. It is now `partial`: *"Take the sign out and there
are still two faces going to two thousand people nobody knows."* Full credit
goes to the audience rule.

**The ending no longer claims what nobody can observe.** It said the two
pictures "never left the tablet". Declining to post is not evidence the app
never received the image, and nothing in the story showed on-device processing.
The pictures are now deleted, Dazzle is closed, and Ms. Okafor says plainly that
you cannot see from the outside what an app does with a picture once it has the
camera — which is why the rule is about what you send and where.

## Found by the fixes

**A fourth public-correction instance.** Sprint 16 banned four exact strings.
This sprint's reading turned up *"Interrupt immediately and publicly"* in The
Spelling Test Surprise, and a coach note in the same mission saying to interrupt
*"out loud, for the whole room"* — both invisible to a guard that matched
literals. The guard now matches the shape: a correcting verb near a public
setting, in either order. It found the fourth instance immediately. My sprint 16
guard was the wrong kind of test and I should have written it this way then.

**A rendering defect.** The teacher preview and the screen-reader announcement
both join a headline to the body with a full stop, and a handful of authored
headlines already ended in one — so the browser check showed *"Nobody said yes
to it.. Those are different things"*. Five headlines across the curriculum had
this, and one ended in a question mark, rendering as *"though?."*. Fixed in the
renderer rather than the content, via `endSentence`, because authors should not
have to remember which kind they wrote. Guarded across every choice.

## Guards added

Thirty in total. **303 tests, up from 273.**

The important one is not a test at all — it is the rule in `validateMission`,
which applies to every mission written from now on rather than to the eight that
existed today.

## What this says about the review process

The oldest open item in this repository is closed, and it closed as a rule
rather than as eight edits. That is the same lesson as sprint 16's escalation
sweep, arriving from the other direction: sprint 16 showed a sweep finding an
unknown defect, and this sprint shows a sweep preventing the next one.

Set against that: sprint 16's public-correction guard, written one sprint ago,
was already too literal to catch a fourth instance sitting in a mission I had
not read. A guard that pins strings pins only what you were looking at. Where
the finding is a shape, match the shape.
