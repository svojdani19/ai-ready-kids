# Sprint 23 review — 27 August 2026

**Scope.** The reporting layer again, and this one is worse than sprint 22.
Sprint 22 was a claim that could mislead a teacher. This is a privacy defect
and a false statement inside the export itself.

Every point in the finding was correct.

---

## What was wrong

### Competency rates were suppressed on the wrong group

Sprint 22 fixed the *numerator and denominator* of the competency rate — it
divides demonstrated pairs by opportunity-bearing pairs now. It did not touch
what the suppression check looked at, which was still `allStudents` at school
level and `students.length` at class level.

So a school with thirty enrolled children, where **one** child had completed the
only mission touching a competency, exported that competency as a percentage.
The contributing group was one. The suppression check saw thirty and passed it.

In grades 2 to 4 a principal or teacher can usually work out who the single
participant was. "Aggregate" is not protection when the aggregate is one child.

### The benchmark path had no suppression at all

`summariseCohortBenchmark` computed fall, spring and matched-growth rates for
any non-empty list — one student included. `buildSchoolReport` passed the object
through unchanged, and `reportToCsv` exported matched growth with no check. A
school with one matched student could export **that child's before-and-after
change**, in a file that simultaneously told the recipient that groups below
five are suppressed.

### The privacy note was therefore untrue

> *"Any group smaller than 5 students is reported as 'too few to report' rather
> than as a percentage."*

That sentence appeared in the report body, the CSV, and the public privacy page.
It was false for exactly the cells most likely to identify a child.

## What changed

**Suppression now counts distinct contributing students, everywhere.**

`summariseCohort` carries `contributorIds` per competency — the students with at
least one recorded opportunity in it. Ids rather than a count, so the school
roll-up can **deduplicate across classes** before checking the threshold, which
it now does. Class cells suppress on that class's own contributors. The type
exposes `contributors` so every surface can show what it counted.

`rateOrSuppress` was renamed in spirit — its parameter is `contributors`, and
its doc comment states the rule and the one deliberate exception rather than
leaving the caller to guess.

**The raw counts go with the rate.** A suppressed cell used to still carry
`demonstrated: 1, possible: 1` on the object, which discloses precisely what the
percentage would have. Both are null when the rate is.

**Benchmark suppression happens at the source**, in `summariseCohortBenchmark`,
not in a view. `preRate` needs five distinct students in that window,
`postRate` five in its own, and `growthPoints` five *matched*. Per-competency
growth uses the same matched threshold, so a cell can never be more revealing
than the headline above it. Every consumer inherits it — the class page, the
admin pages, the report object and the CSV — because a value that exists on the
object is a value that reaches the export.

Participation counts (`matched`, `preCompleted`) stay visible. They say how many
took part, not how anybody did.

**The privacy note now says what is actually true**, in three sentences instead
of one:

- Competency figures need five distinct contributors, and *"contributing means
  having completed a mission that offered the skill, which is usually fewer
  students than are enrolled."*
- Check-in rates need five in that window; growth needs five who did both.
- **Completion rates are named as the exception** and explained, rather than
  being quietly covered by a sentence that did not fit them: there the
  contributing group genuinely is the whole assigned class.

The same correction went to the public privacy page and to the report page's own
description of the competency table, both of which still described the old
denominator.

## Tests

Eight new, covering exactly what the finding asked for:

- Thirty enrolled, one contributor → the cell is null, and the class row is
  **not** marked suppressed, because the roster is fine. Two different things.
- Suppressed cells carry null raw counts too.
- **Boundary:** four contributors null, five reports. Same class, one child
  added between the two assertions.
- **Multi-class deduplication:** three contributors in one class and two in
  another. Neither class reports; the school does, counting five distinct
  children rather than two cells.
- One matched benchmark student in a larger school → every rate and every
  per-competency growth cell null, and the CSV row reads "too few to report"
  with no number anywhere in it.
- Five matched → reports.
- A sweep: **for every suppressed cell in the object, the CSV row must contain
  "too few to report" and no percentage.** The export cannot contradict itself.
- The privacy note must state what the threshold is counted over.

**343 tests, up from 335.**

## What this says about the review process

Sprint 22 fixed the arithmetic of this exact rate and left the suppression check
pointing at the wrong number. I was looking at whether the figure was *true* and
did not ask who it was *about*. Two passes over the same twenty lines, one
defect each, and the second was the more serious.

The general form is worth writing down: **a correctness fix does not carry a
privacy fix with it.** Changing what a number means changes who it identifies,
and the check that guards it has to be re-read in the same breath.

The other thing this sprint is: the first defect found here where the product
made a **written promise it did not keep**. Every earlier finding was something
teaching a child incorrectly, or a dashboard overstating a signal. This one was
a sentence in an export, addressed to a school administrator, asserting a
protection that did not exist for the cells that needed it most. Prose that
makes a guarantee is a test case.

## Deliberately left

- **Nothing verifies the privacy claims against the code that implements them**,
  beyond the specific assertions added here. The notes are still authored prose
  next to a separate implementation, and the two can drift again.
- The CSV suppression sweep covers competency and class-completion rows. It does
  not yet walk every row type generically.
- Every mission has been read once, none twice.
