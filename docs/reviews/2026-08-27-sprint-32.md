# Sprint 32 review — 27 August 2026

**Scope.** Ordinary school workflow, second pass. Both findings correct. Sprint
31 found the first workflow defect; this one found that the product could not
survive its own first renewal.

---

## Finding 1 — there was no school year, only a subscription term

Three related things, and together they mean the annual programme could not roll
over safely.

**No rollover existed.** The classes page took the year from whichever class
sorted first, with `?? "2025-2026"` behind it. The create form hid it in a
hidden field. `createClassAction` had the same literal as its own fallback. **On
27 August 2026 — the day this was written — every new class was still being
created in 2025-2026.** No action anywhere moved the term dates.

**Reports were labelled by an accident of ordering.** `schoolYear` came from
`classes[0]`, so a school with mixed cohorts got whichever row sorted first.

**Retention was anchored to the wrong date.** `purgeDateFor` used
`term_renews_on` — the *subscription renewal* — for every class, while the
interface said "months after the school year ends". In the seed those are
**1 September and 12 June**: three months apart. `class.school_year` already
existed and was ignored. A future cohort would have inherited the same fixed
renewal date and could have come due **before its own retention period had
elapsed**.

### What changed

**They are different fields now.** A school carries `academic_year`,
`year_starts_on` and `year_ends_on` beside the subscription dates, and **every
class snapshots its cohort's `year_ends_on` at creation**. Retention is computed
per class from that snapshot — `purgeDateForClass` — so rolling the school
forward cannot move an existing cohort's deletion date, and a new cohort cannot
inherit an old term's.

The seed keeps the two sets deliberately apart, and a test asserts they differ,
because a seed where they coincided would hide exactly this.

**Class creation takes the year from the school record**, and the form *shows*
it rather than hiding it: *"New classes join the current school year,
2025-2026."* The report takes it from the school too.

**There is a rollover, and it previews before it acts.** `previewRollover` is a
pure function that states all four things the finding asked for: which classes
will be archived, the new year and its dates, that check-ins close so the new
cohort is not handed last year's form, and — as a fact rather than a promise —
that *"Every class keeps the year-end it was created with."* Subscription dates
are explicitly not touched, and the panel says so.

`nextYearLabel` refuses a label it cannot parse rather than guessing, and
`addYear` turns 29 February into 28 February rather than rolling into March.

### Verified in the running app

Rolled Brightwood from 2025-2026 to 2026-2027: four classes archived, the year
advanced, check-ins closed. The 2025-2026 cohorts still read **June 12, 2027**
on the retention page afterwards — their dates did not move, which is the whole
claim.

## Finding 2 — a name could only be fixed by deleting the child

The roster had Add and Remove and nothing between them. A typo, a preferred
name, or two children needing clearer initials left a teacher choosing between a
wrong name on a screen the whole class can see, all year, and deleting the
child — losing every attempt, check-in and badge — then re-adding a blank
record.

`renameStudent` changes `display_name` and nothing else, scoped by class and
student, so the id that attempts, check-ins, badges and any live session all
hang off is untouched. The validation is now a single `validateDisplayName`
shared with adding, so minimisation, length and the full-surname refusal cannot
drift apart between the two paths. Within-class uniqueness excludes the student
being renamed, so correcting `Sammm R.` to `Sam R.` is not blocked by itself.

The audit line names the class and **not the child**, before or after. The form
says out loud that *"Changing a name keeps every mission, check-in and badge"*,
because a teacher who has previously had to delete a child to fix a name will
assume this does the same.

## Tests

Twelve. The year ones cover the seed keeping both date sets apart, retention
counting from the school year and demonstrably *not* from the renewal, mixed
cohorts each right about themselves, and the one that matters: a 2026-2027
cohort run through the purge **in the gap between the old term-derived date and
its own** survives, and goes only when its own date arrives. Plus label parsing,
the leap-day boundary, the preview's four claims, and a full rollover asserting
that no historical deletion date moved and the subscription date did not either.

The rename ones cover a typo corrected with the attempt, evidence, badge and
avatar all intact, a preferred-name change, a colleague's student refused, and
that both actions share one validator.

**440 tests, up from 428.**

## What this says about the review process

Thirty-one sprints ran against a database seeded to a single school year, and
every one of them passed. The defect was not that anything computed wrongly —
it was that **the passage of time had never happened**. There was no second
cohort, no archived year, no August, and nothing in the test suite created one.

That is a whole category the review has not been touching: not "is this correct"
but "is this correct *the second time*". A product sold as an annual
subscription had never been run through a year boundary in any form.

The retention half is also the fourth appearance of a pattern worth naming
plainly by now: **two ideas sharing one field**. Sprint 26 had school membership
doing the work of ownership. Sprint 27 had "fall and spring" with no state
behind it. Sprint 30 had a friendly identifier doing the work of a credential.
Here, the invoice date was doing the work of the last day of school. In each
case the interface described the second idea and the code stored only the first,
and the two coincided closely enough in the demo that nothing looked wrong.

## Deliberately left

- **No migrations.** Adding a column needs `data/airk.db` deleted, because
  `db:reset` truncates in place so a running dev server survives it. Fine for a
  demo, documented in the README, and a real deployment needs migrations.
- **Rollover is one school at a time and archives everything in the current
  year.** A school running two cohorts side by side is not modelled.
