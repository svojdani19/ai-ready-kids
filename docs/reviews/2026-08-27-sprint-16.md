## The two systemic findings

These are the ones worth reading if you read nothing else, because each turned
up in more than one mission and neither was visible one mission at a time.

### Teacher guidance told teachers to correct a child in front of the class

**The Practice That Got Skipped** on a child who says they are slow at times
tables: *"Interrupt it publicly"* and *"Interrupt this every time."*
**The Reading Log** on a child with a low number: *"Respond to a low number with
curiosity in front of the class, once, and the logs change."*

Both were written with good intent — the first fights a fixed-ability belief,
the second normalises honesty. Both single out one child, in front of everyone,
for something that usually tracks a learning difference or what is happening at
home. A child who says "I am just slow at times tables" may be describing
dyscalculia. A child with a four in the reading box may have been up with a
baby. Neither is the teacher's to surface publicly, however kindly meant.

Both now say to take it privately, and both say why. The reading log guidance
adds the alternative: normalise the range in general terms — *this week's logs
have fours and forties in them and both are useful* — which achieves the same
thing without naming anybody.

### The slow-recall mission promised an outcome it cannot deliver

Related, and worse. **The Practice That Got Skipped** ran: practise four nights,
and on Friday "seven eights comes out of your mouth before you have decided to
say it." Student copy said the nine seconds "turns into one second". The guide
said to interrupt any child who claims to be slow.

Together that teaches a child that the only reason anybody stays slow is that
they skipped the practice. For a child with dyscalculia, a working-memory
difference, ADHD or real maths anxiety, practising properly for a fortnight and
still being slow then means something is wrong with *them* — which is worse than
not teaching it at all.

Practice now "makes it shorter", and "how much shorter, and how quickly, is
different for everybody, and it does not move at all if you skip it." A fourth
choice was added to Wednesday: **do them slowly, and tell Ms. Okafor they are
still really hard** — full credit, because a child for whom practice is not
working needs an action rather than more encouragement. The wrap-up carries it.
The setup says the story is "a story rather than a promise", names the
conditions, and adds the thing nobody had said: **some children have a
calculator by agreement, as an accommodation, and this mission is not about
them** — worth saying before you start rather than leaving them to work it out.

## The rest, mission by mission

**The Group Project** gave its four children four neat talents and then asked
real classes to do the same: *"have each group write down what each member is
bringing."* A child who cannot name anything sits in front of a blank line. The
extension now starts from **the jobs the task needs** — reading it out, checking
spelling, timekeeping, deciding the order, holding the ruler — and the group
allocates. "The whole reason there are four of you is that you can do four
different things" became "there are four of you so that four people end up
having done something."

**It Happened to Theo** was the bystander mission, and it stopped where sprint
15 predicted: Theo tells Ms. Okafor, she is kind, she tells his grown-up — and
**nobody does anything about the address he typed in.** She now looks for where
it went and takes it out, writes down what she cannot be sure about, and speaks
to his grown-up the same night so they can decide the rest together. New
wrap-up line: *telling somebody is the start. Something has to be taken out or
turned off as well.*

**The Weather Argument** asserted, as an easy first-hand check, that the class
fish is awake: *"He is awake. He is always awake."* Fish rest, and having no
eyelids is exactly why looking does not settle it — so the example was both
false and, on the mission's own terms, in the wrong column. It now asks whether
Captain is swimming or on the gravel, and the feedback turns the error into the
lesson: *"Whether he is asleep is a much harder question, because fish have no
eyelids to close. Where he is, though, is four metres away."* The extension uses
it to show that two questions about the same thing can sit in different columns.
Also: *"First-hand beats forecast, every time"* is an absolute the mission
itself disproves four scenes later, and is now "For right now, the window wins."

**The Science Fair Fact** is strong and the myth checks out. One line —
*"If none of them can say, you have nothing"* — now reads "you cannot use it
yet", matching the fix made to The Book That Was Not There in sprint 13. And its
ending said the poster "does not win either", which referred to another
mission's ending; the missions are interleaved, so nothing guarantees a child
has met it.

**The Story That Was Not Mine** promised that your own worse writing "stops
being worse surprisingly quickly" — the same unkeepable timeline as the times
tables. It now "gets better the more of them you write". It also taught what to
ask for and never that you say what you asked for, which is odd next to The Art
Show Label and the new Perfect Drawing, both of which make disclosure the norm.
Added to the wrap-up.

**The Art Show Label** had no findings. It is the model the other ownership
missions are being pulled towards.

## The sweep, and the ninth mission

The escalation finding generalised, so I wrote it as a sweep over every mission
whose primary skill is `privacy.escalate`: the story must show something being
stopped, removed, turned off or handed on, and somebody outside the room being
told.

It failed on **The Question at Bedtime** — one of the original nine, and the one
the guide itself calls "the most important". An AI assistant on a school tablet
drifts into personal questions and offers a child secrecy from their parents.
The child closes the tablet and tells a grown-up. The mission then ends with a
poster of trusted adults on the wall, and **nothing whatsoever happens to
AskMe.** Every other child in the school still has it.

The mission's own guide already knew: its misconception response says
*"Reporting protects the next child."* The story just never delivered it. It now
does, quietly: the grown-up tells the school, the school looks, and by Thursday
that part of AskMe is switched off on every tablet in the building. Nobody makes
an announcement. New wrap-up line: **telling one grown-up is how it gets fixed
for everybody, not just for you.**

## Guards added

Eight tests. Two are curriculum-wide sweeps rather than mission-specific pins,
which is the first time a content guard has generalised this way:

- **No mission's teacher guidance** — setup, misconception responses or coach
  notes — may contain instructions to address an individual child's shortfall
  publicly.
- **Every escalation mission** must show something stopped, removed or handed
  on, and somebody outside the room told. This is the one that found the ninth
  mission, and it will catch the next one.

Plus five phrases added to the brittle-absolutes ban list: `he is always awake`,
`turns into one second`, `stops being worse surprisingly quickly`,
`first-hand beats forecast, every time`.

273 tests, up from 265.

## What this says about the review process

Eighteen of eighteen Sprint 09 missions have now been read. **Seventeen had
findings.** The one that did not is The Art Show Label.

Two things changed in how I read this time. First, reading eight at once made
the systemic findings visible — the public-correction advice appears twice, the
unkeepable timeline three times, and neither would have been more than a
quibble in a single mission. Second, writing the escalation finding as a sweep
rather than a pin immediately found a mission outside the batch I was reading.
That is the first guard in this repository that discovered something rather
than pinning something already known.

The accumulated checklist did most of the work. Every finding this sprint maps
to an item added in sprints 11–15 — which is the first evidence that the
checklist is worth what it cost to build.

## Deliberately left

- **The eight legacy forced-award scenes**, and their untraced interaction with
  the coaching rule. This is now the oldest open item in the repository.
- **The original nine missions have never had a systematic read.** Sprint 16
  touched one of them, by accident, through a sweep. That is not coverage.
- Teacher-facing copy across several missions promises timelines it cannot
  support — "the habit sets in about two rounds faster than expected", "the
  first-column reaches drop off within days", "see what changes within a
  fortnight". Harmless to children, mildly dishonest to teachers, not fixed.
